## Key question 1: Which documented campaigns (any industrial/energy/hardware B2B sector) used unconventional tactics to reach senior execs, and what were the measured results?

### Takeaway
Rigorous, primary-sourced industrial B2B guerrilla case studies are scarce; the best-documented "unconventional" B2B results are vendor-reported ABM/direct-mail case studies (Sendoso: Terminus, Gainsight, Zendesk, Anaplan) and a handful of agency-roundup anecdotes (Aladdin Knowledge Systems 35% meeting rate; Canon oversized C-suite packages; a $22k non-sponsor street campaign that lifted inbound conference meeting requests 40%). ## Key question 2: What do experienced cleantech/energy sales practitioners say actually got them first pilots?

### Takeaway
Practitioner content converges on: treat the pilot as a paid commercial relationship with the P&L owner (not the innovation team) in the room from day one, quantify the cost of doing nothing, pre-schedule the go/no-go and refuse free extensions, and bring the buyer a ready-made pilot design (for cleaning: alternating cleaned vs control rows, irradiance-corrected PR, weather-adjusted baseline). No SunCast / Solar Maverick episode transcripts with first-pilot lessons were retrievable in this session; one Watt It Takes listing is an anecdote (3Degrees founder driving rice fields to land a first customer).

### Cited Findings
- "Pilot purgatory" in climate tech is "the structural condition where a climate tech company successfully completes pilot after pilot but cannot convert any of them into commercial contracts"; causes include a pilot team built for bespoke delivery rather than repeatable sales and buyers with no procurement path for the category — [Rebel Strategy Lab](https://rebelstrategylab.com/diagnosis/pilot-purgatory/).
- Utility pilots stall when "the business blocks new ideas because they are too risky to pilot or the idea makes it to the field but never lands"; sponsors leaving their roles can strand a pilot's value — [Latitude Media, "How utilities can escape pilot purgatory"](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/pilot-purgatory-the-quintessential-definition-of-insanity/).
- Engage the budget owner early: innovation teams often pay for pilots but rarely own the plant P&L, so bring the operations leader in as early as possible; document the current cost of the problem; schedule the go/no-go before the pilot ends; treat an extension request at the decision gate as "the first step into purgatory" — [Traction Technology, "How to Run a Successful Startup Pilot"](https://www.tractiontechnology.com/blog/how-to-run-a-successful-pilot-with-a-startup-frameworks-kpis-enterprise-best-practices); see also [Carl Vause, "What is Pilot Purgatory"](https://carl-vause.medium.com/what-is-pilot-purgatory-and-why-every-start-up-should-know-this-term-93136ffcfb1a).
- A paid pilot, even discounted, "is already a commercial relationship"; founders should hire an experienced enterprise salesperson early (enterprise-software playbook applied to climate tech) — [Climate & Capital Media, "Crossing the valley of death"](https://www.climateandcapitalmedia.com/crossing-the-valley-of-death-enterprise-softwares-playbook-for-climate-tech/); related: [Drew Kriens, "Escaping Pilot Purgatory"](https://drewkriens.substack.com/p/escaping-pilot-purgatory) (neither page could be fetched; summary from search snippets).
- Hardware-founder mentor Tom McLeod: design the pilot for the customer rather than waiting for them to define it, and bring large enterprises a ready-made proposal because "they can get lost in requirements" — [Startmate](https://www.startmate.com/writing/hardware-founder-leader-tom-mcleod-on-building-hustling-early-and-keeping-founders-grounded).
- 2026 climate-tech founder playbook: use no-code tools, spreadsheets, manual/concierge services before building the full product; buyers want "numbers, compliance fit, audit trails, downtime reduction, or lower waste bills" — [Jeffrey Paine, "Climate Tech in 2026"](https://jeffreypaine.com/climate-tech-in-2026-the-founders-playbook).
- Sightline Climate's FOAK guide (project-scale, but relevant): pilots must prove the technology works in the field before FOAK financing — [Sightline FOAK Guide 2024](https://decarbtechinvest.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Sightline-Climate-FOAK-Guide-2024-1.pdf); scaling keys for climate hardware — [Speedinvest](https://speedinvest.ghost.io/5-keys-to-scaling-your-climate-tech-hardware-startup/); lessons from Cleantech 1.0 — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/eight-lessons-from-the-first-climate-tech-boom-and-bust).
- Watt It Takes (Powerhouse, host Emily Kirsch) is "an in-depth interview with a successful clean energy entrepreneur"; one episode listing describes the 3Degrees founder "driving through California rice fields blasting Eminem to land his first customer" (anecdote; deal details not in listing) — [Apple Podcasts listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/watt-it-takes/id1554962073); [Powerhouse](https://powerhouse-ventures.co/watt-it-takes/emily-kirsch).
- Solar-cleaning pilot design advice: "Price both capex and lease if available, and insist on a pilot with alternating cleaned versus control rows to validate energy uplift" — [Cotech Solar](https://cotechsolar.com/guides/do-solar-cleaning-robots-pay-off/); "Tie payment to measured performance-ratio recovery and documented cycle completion rather than visits performed" — [Market Research Future](https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/solar-panel-cleaning-market-21588) (market report; unsourced); cleaning-contract penalties "typically structured as liquidated damages calculated based on the energy yield shortfall" against a weather-adjusted baseline — [Taypro, "Performance Guarantee Clauses in Solar Cleaning Contracts"](https://taypro.in/blog/performance-guarantee-clauses-in-solar-cleaning-contracts) (vendor).
- Measurement: compare irradiance-corrected PR before/after; note that "a performance guarantee that assumes a cleaning regime makes it an obligation rather than an economic choice, and failing it can void a claim regardless of merit"; in wet temperate climates "rain removes much of the soiling for nothing"; one case measured >15% yield gain right after cleaning that fell below reference within ~two months — [ETMAX, "Cleaning, and Whether It Pays"](https://etmaxdoo.com/operating/cleaning/).
- A startup business-plan template suggests "first secure one paid 90-day pilot with an IPP or SAM targeting a verifiable minimum 3% AEP uplift" — [BusinessPlan-Templates](https://businessplan-templates.com/blogs/start-business/solar-panel-cleaning) (template, not evidence).
- Solarplaza resource "PV Module Cleaning: Does It Pay Back?" exists as a practitioner reference — [Solarplaza](https://www.solarplaza.com/resource/11400/pv-module-cleaning-does-it-pay-back/) (not fetchable; content not reviewed).

### Inferences
- For a self-cleaning product (coating/robot/electrostatic), the "first pilot" lever is a pre-designed, low-friction pilot package: 1–2 strings/rows treated, adjacent control rows, 60–90 days, PR/irradiance-corrected comparison, a named O&M head as sponsor plus the asset manager who owns the P&L, and a go/no-go date written into the pilot MoU. This directly addresses the sponsor-turnover and "free extension" failure modes practitioners describe.
- The two-months-to-fade observation (ETMAX) is an argument for the vendor: a self-cleaning surface's value is the integral over the soiling cycle, so pilots should run across at least one dry-season soiling cycle and be measured continuously, not with a single before/after snapshot.

- No SunCast, Solar Maverick, Elemental, Cleantech Group, YC/Techstars-energy or Solarplaza speaker content specifically on "what got us our first pilot" was retrievable; the podcasts' episode pages were not fetchable.
- No documented "no-yield-gain, no-fee" solar cleaning contract was found; only general contract guidance and a template.

## Key question 3: Which tactics are specifically risky or illegal around solar plants, and how are they executed safely with permission?

### Takeaway
Drone imagery of plants is lawful only with the right certificate, airspace clearance and (in practice) asset-owner consent: India's Drone Rules 2021 (as amended 2022/2023) zone airspace via DigitalSky and many solar parks sit in yellow/red zones; the FAA does not require landowner consent for overflight but Part 107, over-people limits and state privacy/trespass law still apply. Physical site access requires owner approval, induction and (for any work on equipment) a permit-to-work; unapproved cleaning methods can void module warranties (First Solar requires cleaning-system approval; Jinko bans chemicals/abrasives). For outreach, CAN-SPAM is opt-out, GDPR needs a documented legitimate-interest basis, and India's DPDP consent obligations are expected to bite from ~May 2027 with no legitimate-interest route and TCCCPR covering calls/SMS.

### Cited Findings

Drones, India (DGCA / Drone Rules 2021)
- Drone Rules 2021 (Ministry of Civil Aviation) liberalised the regime — [PHDCCI copy of MoCA release](https://www.phdcci.in/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Ministry-of-Civil-Aviation-notifies-liberalised-Drone-Rules-2021.pdf). The Rules were amended in February 2022 and September 2023, and the Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam, 2024 now governs alongside them — [Neeti Niyaman, "Drone Laws in India"](https://neetiniyaman.com/drone-laws-in-india/).
- Airspace is zoned green/yellow/red on the DigitalSky platform; no permission in green zones, clearance needed in yellow, and red zones need specific authorisation from MHA/DGCA — [Kodainya, Drone Rules 2021](https://www.kodainya.com/blogs/drone-rules-2021); [Axesmap](https://blog.axesmap.com/dgca-drone-rules-india-survey/) (one guide says submit a flight plan via DigitalSky at least 24 hours before flying in yellow zones).
- Solar parks "near airstrips, refineries, industrial corridors, or defence facilities frequently enter this category of restricted or permission-required airspace" — [Kodainya, drones for solar inspection](https://www.kodainya.com/blogs/drones-for-solar-panel-inspection).
- Get "written permission from asset owner — power utility, plant operator, or property owner before flying over infrastructure" — [Elion, DGCA rules for industrial inspection/thermography](https://elion.co.in/legal-and-regulatory-considerations-for-drone-thermography-operations/).
- Commercial operations need a Remote Pilot Certificate; Rule 44 makes third-party insurance mandatory — [RobotWale](https://www.robotwale.com/article/dgca-drone-rules-india-2024-compliance-guide); [Zbotic 2026 guide](https://zbotic.in/dgca-drone-rules-india-2026-registration-license-guide/); [Asteria](https://asteria.co.in/blog/indias-new-drone-regulations/). (All commercial/consultancy blogs; the search found no DGCA rule specific to solar plants; check DigitalSky and current DGCA notifications.)

Drones, US (FAA Part 107)
- The FAA "does not require property owner consent for overflight"; the general ceiling is 400 ft AGL in uncontrolled airspace; there is no FAA "floor" over private land; the focus is on safe conduct — [UAV Coach](https://uavcoach.com/drone-over-private-property/); [Airsight](https://www.airsight.com/blog/drone-over-private-property-law).
- Part 107 restricts flights over uninvolved people absent exceptions/waivers; a remote pilot certificate is required for commercial work — [JOUAV state-by-state guide](https://www.jouav.com/blog/can-you-fly-a-drone-over-private-property.html); [Drone U state laws](https://www.thedroneu.com/blog/usa-drone-laws-regulations-by-state/).
- State/local law is the main caveat: several states (e.g., California, Texas) restrict drone photography over residential property below certain altitudes; privacy laws can be violated even without overflight — [CriminalDefenseLawyer.com](https://www.criminaldefenselawyer.com/resources/when-a-drone-flies-over-my-house-is-it-trespassing.html); [Legally Explained](https://legallyexplained.com/is-it-legal-to-fly-drones-over-private-property/). No source addressed solar farms specifically.

- PVRADAR is described as "a software platform for transparent soiling assessment from satellite data and seamless cleaning optimization for utility-scale PV" — [Terrapinn speaker bio](https://www.terrapinn.com/exhibition/solar-storage-live-zurich/speaker-thore-MÜLLER.stm) (status/customers unverified).

It must be in place before any work starts on-site"; "the Asset Owner has ultimate legal and moral responsibility for ensuring the health and safety of people in and around the solar plant" — [Solar Best Practices, Health & Safety Management](https://solarbestpractices.com/guidelines/detail/health-and-safety-management).
- Contractors/technicians are high-risk visitors needing permit checks, competency review, PPE and possibly escort; office guests need lighter controls — [OHSE.ca](https://ohse.ca/visitor-safety-procedures-start-before-anyone-arrives-on-site/).
- Even unlawful trespassers' injuries may be deemed the landowner's responsibility if access was not prevented; missing fencing/signage can create public-liability claims — [Clearway](https://www.clearway.co.uk/news/the-rise-in-solar-panels-thefts-protecting-your-solar-farm/); [WCCTV solar farm security guide](https://www.wcctv.co.uk/the-ultimate-guide-to-solar-farm-security/); permitted visitors vs trespassers are treated differently under English occupiers' liability law — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupiers%27_liability_in_English_law); solar leases typically shift liability to the developer via indemnities — [National Ag Law Center](https://nationalaglawcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/assets/articles/hall_solar_Leasing.pdf).

Email, messaging and data-protection law
- CAN-SPAM (US) applies to all commercial email incl. B2B; no opt-in needed; requires working opt-out, physical address, and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days; per-email penalties cited at $46,517 (FTC 2024) or $53,088 (16 CFR 1.98) depending on source — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/b2b-email-list-compliance-gdpr-canspam/); [Modern Inbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/cold-email-compliance-guide-2026); [Stealery](https://www.getstealery.com/blog/cold-outreach/cold-email-compliance-guide).
- GDPR (EU/UK): B2B cold email generally permitted on a documented legitimate-interest basis (Art. 6(1)(f)) passing the three-part test, with clear opt-out; the basis "is genuinely debated among privacy lawyers, not a settled green light" and is not applied uniformly across member states; max fine €20M or 4% of global revenue — [Grou Global](https://grouglobal.com/blog/gdpr-cold-email-the-operator-playbook-for-b2b-teams); [Overloop](https://overloop.com/blog/cold-email-illegal); [GrowthList](https://growthlist.co/gdpr-cold-email/); Canada's CASL is stricter (consent model) — [OutreachBloom](https://outreachbloom.com/cold-email-compliance).
- India DPDP Act 2023: most obligations apply from ~May 2027 (one guide: 13 May 2027; rules notified November 2025 with an 18-month phased timeline); the Act has no legitimate-interest basis and its "legitimate uses" do not include marketing, so outreach will generally need consent; a corporate email containing an individual's name is treated as personal data (the "B2B is exempt" belief is described as a myth); penalties up to ₹250 crore; historical email enforcement has been very low; there is no Indian anti-spam email statute, and TRAI's TCCCPR 2018 covers voice calls and SMS over telecom networks — [Ken.so, "DPDP Act: What It Means for Cold Email"](https://ken.so/cold-email-laws/dpdp-india); [NodGuard](https://nodguard.in/email-marketing-dpdp/); [PuzzleInbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-india-dpdp-act-2026/); [B2B Data Index 44-nation matrix](https://b2bdataindex.com/compliance/); [Aqua Consento](https://www.aquaconsento.com/blog/dpdp-marketing-strategies). One guide claims cold email is legal in India under a "legitimate purpose basis for B2B" — [Cold Outbound Playbook](https://coldoutboundplaybook.com/guides/is-cold-email-legal-in-india/) — which conflicts with the others and appears unreliable.
- WhatsApp: obtain explicit opt-in before first contact; use the official WhatsApp Business API and Meta's template-approval policy rather than unofficial bulk tools; regulated buyers (banking, healthcare, government) may prohibit WhatsApp for sales — [Vedain](https://vedain.com/blog/how-to-use-whatsapp-for-b2b-sales-in-india-a-complete-guide); [Nummero](https://www.nummero.com/feeds/whatsapp-b2b-marketing-india).

### Inferences
- Exclude outright: unauthorized drone flights over plants (both jurisdictions, and India's yellow/red zoning makes many parks non-green), any entry past a plant fence without owner approval and induction, touching modules/strings without a permit-to-work and the owner's isolation procedures, scraped personal-data prospect lists for India/EU, WhatsApp cold blasts, and any "free cleaning" using chemicals/abrasives/pressure that breaches module OEM guidance (warranty exposure for the customer, liability for the vendor).
- Safe substitutes that keep the "visual hook": (a) satellite or public NREL/Solargis-style soiling data as the outreach hook instead of drone images; (b) drone/thermal only after a signed site-access and overflight consent, with a certified pilot (RPC / Part 107), insurance and DigitalSky/FAA airspace check; (c) demo on a vendor-owned portable rig or a partner's test bed (NISE/IIT/university rooftop) instead of on the prospect's live array; (d) "cleaning one row" only under the plant's own permit-to-work with the O&M contractor executing or supervising.
- Email strategy by region: US = opt-out compliant cold email allowed; EU/UK = legitimate-interest assessment on file + unsubscribe; India = move to consent-first now (events, LinkedIn, referrals, WhatsApp opt-in) so the funnel does not break at the May 2027 DPDP deadline, and never cold-SMS/call on Indian numbers without TCCCPR-compliant registration.

## Key question 5: What is a realistic 90-day guerrilla go-to-market plan and budget for a small vendor, sequenced from cheapest/highest-leverage to most expensive?

### Takeaway
Evidence supports a sequence of (weeks 1–4) free data-led assets and consent-based account mapping, (weeks 3–8) proof-first pilot offers and ecosystem letters, (weeks 6–12) a conference-adjacent hosted event plus a small physical-mailer wave to 20–40 named O&M/asset-management heads; practitioner sources argue the pilot itself should be paid or at least commercially structured with a go/no-go date. Costs below are the researcher's estimates (unsourced) except where a cited anchor exists.

### Cited Findings
- Cost anchors from sources: a non-sponsor conference street campaign cost ~$22,000 and lifted inbound meeting requests 40% — [American Guerrilla Marketing](https://americanguerrillamarketing.com/articles/guerrilla-marketing-case-studies/) (anecdote); personalized direct mail is tracked through Sendoso–Salesforce integration and reported pipeline multiples in vendor case studies — [Sendoso: Gainsight](https://www.sendoso.com/success-stories/gainsight), [Zendesk](https://www.sendoso.com/success-stories/zendesk).

### Inferences (90-day plan; budget figures are estimates, not sourced)
- Days 1–15 (near-zero cash, high leverage): build a target-account map of 50 plants/owners (IPPs, utilities, EPC-O&M arms, independent O&M firms) using public tender/COD news (e.g., NTPC Bhadla/Khavda tranches, SECI projects); publish a "Regional Soiling Loss Index" page built from public NREL/Solargis/satellite methods with per-cluster loss ranges (Bhadla/Jodhpur, Khavda/Bhuj, Pavagada, plus US Southwest/MENA); open a free ROI calculator; begin a 2–3x/week LinkedIn posting cadence on soiling economics and comment substantively on O&M leaders' posts. Estimated cost: founder time + ~$0–500 (hosting, design templates).
- Days 10–40 (low cash): offer a "free soiling audit" that uses only public/satellite data plus the owner's SCADA export (no site entry, no drones) producing a 2-page plant-specific loss estimate; send 20–40 hyper-personalized ROI one-pagers (by email where lawful: US opt-out, EU legitimate interest; by warm intro/LinkedIn/WhatsApp-after-consent in India); record 60–90-second personalized videos for the top 20 accounts; request warranty-compatibility statements from 2–3 module OEMs and tracker OEMs; line up one independent engineer or institute (NREL-style lab, Fraunhofer, IIT/NISE in India) for a paid or co-funded verification plan. Estimated cost: ~$1–3k (video tool, data tooling, legal review of outreach templates).
- Days 30–70 (moderate cash): sign 1–2 risk-reversal pilots ("no measured yield gain, no fee" or discounted paid pilot with LD-style terms) on alternating rows under the plant's permit-to-work with the O&M contractor executing; pre-agree the measurement protocol (irradiance-corrected PR, control rows, dry-season window) and a go/no-go date; run a practitioner roundtable (8–12 O&M heads) either online or as a dinner in Delhi/Jaipur/Ahmedabad (or Phoenix/Madrid for US/EU). Estimated cost: ~$3–10k (pilot hardware/consumables, travel, dinner).
- Days 60–90 (highest cash): conference-adjacent play around Bharat RE Summit (Nov) or Intersolar India (Feb) / RE+ — hosted dinner for 15–25 asset managers, booth-adjacent demo rig in a partner's stand or a nearby hotel suite, plus a small physical mailer wave (e.g., a soiling-sample vial from the prospect's own region with the ROI card) to 20–40 O&M/asset-management heads; publish the first pilot's before/after dataset with the independent engineer's sign-off and pitch it as a trade-press op-ed/podcast topic. Estimated cost: ~$8–25k (dinner, suite, mailers, travel), i.e., still well under the ~$22k street-campaign anecdote plus booth costs.
- Total 90-day envelope: roughly $15–40k cash plus founder time; the sequence front-loads zero-cost data assets because Edelman's data suggests credible, data-rich content is what moves executives and because India's consent regime rewards inbound.
