Research method note: findings below come from three rounds of parallel web searches (pv magazine, PV Tech, Mercom, Solar Quarter, Globes, KAUST, NREL, BU, company releases, aggregators). ## Key Question 1: Who are the established and emerging players in each cleaning-tech category (HQ, scale, major customers)?

### Takeaway
Waterless autonomous row robots are the only category with multi-GW commercial scale, and leadership has shifted since 2023 from the Israeli pioneers (Ecoppia, Airtouch) to Chinese manufacturers (Sunpure, Leapting) and an Indian local champion (Taypro) selling to Adani, Waaree, BluPine, Tata Power, ACWA Power and others; coatings (DSM/Covestro, Pellucere), electrodynamic screens (Sol Clarity, CleanFizz), vibration/acoustic (Fraunhofer IDMT, university labs) and drones (SolarDrone/VisionWave, Apellix) remain pilot-scale or niche.

## Key Question 2: Which business models are winning (capex sale vs robots-as-a-service vs performance-guaranteed) and what price points are public?

### Takeaway
Capex hardware sale under master supply/framework agreements (Airtouch-Adani, Leapting-Waaree/Adani/BluPine, Sunpure-Jakson/ACWA) is the dominant utility-scale model; OPEX "pay-per-verified-panel-cleaned" exists (Taypro) and the per-panel cost advantage over manual cleaning (~Rs 0.20 vs Rs 1-1.2 per panel) is the standard sales argument, but no vendor publishes a per-MW-per-year RaaS price or a performance guarantee.

### Cited Findings
- Taypro: offers both CAPEX and OPEX; OPEX "customers pay in line with verified panels cleaned each month rather than buying robots outright" — [Taypro OPEX page](https://taypro.in/solar-panel-cleaning-system/solar-panel-cleaning-service).
- Airtouch-Adani: $2.5m (May 2023) and $6m (Apr 2024) orders under a "master supply agreement" that "lets AGEL place additional orders... in the future" (capex sale, supply + install) — [pv magazine India, Apr 2024](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2024/04/04/airtouch-solar-secures-6-million-cleaning-robot-contract-with-adani-green-energy/).
- Leapting-Waaree: 2,000+ robots for 1.2 GWp on 2P fixed tilt (≈1.7 robots/MW) — supply contract — [pv magazine India, Jan 2026](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/01/08/leapting-to-deploy-2000-g1-cleaning-robots-across-waaree-renewables-1-2-gwp-solar-projects/).
- Per-panel economics (India): "robotic cleaning typically costs about ₹0.20 (~$0.0022) per panel, compared to ₹1 (~$0.011) to ₹1.2 (~$0.013) per panel for manual cleaning"; manual "requires 7–12 tons of water per MW for each cycle" — [Mercom](https://mercomindia.com/robotic-cleaning-solar-modules-gaining-traction-india).
- Manual cleaning cost benchmark: "annual cleaning costs up to Rs 750,000 per MW per year"; water "as high as 24,000 litres per MW for a single cycle" — [Renewable Watch, Oct 2023](https://renewablewatch.in/2023/10/01/efficiency-gains-solutions-for-solar-panel-maintenance/). A lower figure, "as high as Rs 1 lakh for every MW" for plants using surface water — [IAMRenew](https://www.iamrenew.com/policy/mnre-asks-for-robots-to-cut-water-usage-at-solar-parks/).
- Robot capex: "a robotic system that comes in at Rs 10 Lakhs-20 Lakhs (Ecoppia Systems)" (unit basis unclear); Ecoppia's Dadri 5 MW lifetime figure works out to ~Rs 15.2 lakh per MW over 25 years vs traditional O&M "at least Rs 500,000 MW/year and goes up to Rs 800,000 MW/year" (vendor claim) — [IAMRenew](https://www.iamrenew.com/policy/mnre-asks-for-robots-to-cut-water-usage-at-solar-parks/).
- Small-plant figure: "about Rs 5 lakh per annum for a 5 MW plant with a robotic system... almost 10 times lower than traditional methods of water-based cleaning" (vendor-sourced) — [YourStory (Indibots)](https://yourstory.com/companies/indibots).
- Robot density drives price: "16 robots per MW on a retrofit and as few as 1–2 per MW on greenfield projects" (Ecoppia) — [Mercom interview](https://mercomindia.com/greenfield-projects-amenable-robotic-cleaning). Earlier 2019-2020 market overviews — [Renewable Watch 2019](https://renewablewatch.in/2019/06/29/robots-at-work/); [Renewable Watch 2020](https://renewablewatch.in/2020/12/04/robots-at-work-2/); buyer checklist — [pv magazine India, Nov 2022](https://pv-magazine-india.com/2022/11/09/key-factors-when-choosing-an-automated-cleaning-system-for-utility-scale-pv).
- Valuation signals: Ecoppia business sold for NIS 18m in 2024 vs NIS 1.3bn IPO value — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001473387); Junko Solar (services) valued at $400,000 in 2026 — [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/visionwave-subsidiary-solardrone-acquires-controlling-interest-junko-solar-and).
- Tractor-mounted economics are pitched on throughput: SunBrush TrackFlex "10,000 square meters per hour" — [WFES exhibitor page](https://wfes.german-pavilion.com/en/sites/exhibitors/112904).
- Coating business model: DSM/Covestro sell factory-applied coatings to module/glass makers (B2B component) and marketed a retrofit AR coating — [DSM TechTalk deck](https://solar-media.s3.amazonaws.com/assets/Pubs/Webinars/DSM%20TechTalk%200909/DSM%20TechTalk%20webinar%20presentation%200909.pdf); Pellucere sells field application of MoreSun to asset owners — [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/pellucere-technologies-offers-worlds-first-field-installable-anti-reflectiv/).
- EDS business model: Sol Clarity offers retrofit overlay sheets or OEM integration — [BU](https://www.bu.edu/eng/2024/12/06/renewable-power-without-the-power-washing).
- US contracting context (market-report claim, low reliability): "as of 2024, 65% of all utility-scale contracts in the United States contain specific clauses requiring documented cleaning intervals" — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/power-plant/top-drone-solar-panel-maintenance-solutions-for-power-generation-2026).

### Inferences
- The India price war is visible in orders of 2,000 robots per ~1 GWp: at the Rs 10-20 lakh/unit figures quoted for Ecoppia circa 2019, such orders would be worth $25-50m, which is inconsistent with the $2.5-6m Airtouch orders for comparable Adani sites; unit prices in 2025-26 are almost certainly far below the 2019 benchmarks, though no current unit price was found.
- Taypro's per-verified-panel OPEX model is the only documented "cleaning-as-a-service" structure; it aligns vendor revenue with cleaning events rather than energy yield, so no vendor appears to carry yield/performance risk.
- Developer-side framework agreements (Adani with Airtouch and Leapting; Waaree; BluPine) indicate buyers prefer to qualify a vendor once and then call off per project; a new entrant should design a framework-agreement template rather than one-off quotes.

### Gaps
- No published per-MW/year price for robots-as-a-service or any performance-guaranteed cleaning contract was found.
- No current (2024-2026) unit prices for Chinese G1/Sunpure robots or Taypro robots.
- Airtouch and Ecoppia audited revenue and gross-margin figures (available in TASE filings) were not retrieved.

## Key Question 3: How did Ecoppia, Airtouch, NOMADD, SolarCleano, Taypro (and the Chinese entrants) land early customers and scale?

### Takeaway
The pioneers' first wins came from a home-market champion willing to host a pilot (Arava Power/Siemens' Ketura Sun for Ecoppia; Enlight for Airtouch; KAUST/Aramco/NEOM-SATCO for NOMADD), followed by one anchor developer in the target market (Adani for Airtouch, Bhadla park/NTPC-era tenders for Ecoppia) converted into a master supply agreement; Chinese entrants scaled by local sales teams signing framework deals with Indian IPPs at trade expos (REI) and by riding ACWA Power's Saudi gigaprojects.

- Pilot at a friendly home-market site, then full roll-out in <3 months: Ketura Sun (Arava Power/Siemens), ~100 E4 robots, 2014 — [PR Newswire 2014](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ecoppia-announces-worlds-first-completely-autonomously-cleaned-solar-energy-park-251939791.html); [TechXplore, Apr 2014](https://techxplore.com/news/2014-04-negev-solar-field-water-free-robotic.html).

## Key Question 4: What validation do buyers demand before adopting (independent engineers, module-OEM compatibility, insurance, IEC standards)?

### Takeaway
The buyer-side concern that dominates is module damage from brush abrasion (AR-coating loss, microcracks): PI Berlin, QEERI/HBKU, Fraunhofer and DEWA have all published robot-vs-module studies, IEC 62788-7-3:2022 (amended 2024) now standardises abrasion tests, and module OEMs (JinkoSolar; First Solar certification of Ecoppia) and tracker OEMs (Nextracker compatibility letters) issue vendor-specific approvals — so a new entrant needs a module-OEM compatibility letter and an IEC 62788-7-3-based abrasion test report before developers will adopt.

### Cited Findings
- "Cleaning-robots can cause long-term module damage, finds PI Berlin" (headline; study content not retrieved) — [PV Tech](https://pv-tech.org/cleaning-robots-can-cause-long-term-module-damage-finds-pi-berlin).
- "PV cleaning robots: new test methods for a new technology": "Two open standards were published this year, and at least one PV module manufacturer is developing its own in-house test protocol"; proposed approval-transfer logic: "if a robot is already approved to be used with module X, and a standard test shows that module Y has the same abrasion resistance as module X, then the robot could be safely approved for module Y" — [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/pv-cleaning-robots-new-test-methods-for-a-new-technology/).
- A field study (QEERI/HBKU publications) found abrasion effects are module-specific: "five kinds of module showed greater losses with more frequent cleaning, while the other four did not", with authors recommending field testing of specific robot/module pairs — [HBKU: Real-world performance and module degradation impacts of robotic cleaning](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/real-world-performance-and-module-degradation-impacts-of-robotic-/); [HBKU: Abrasion of PV antireflective coatings by robot cleaning](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/abrasion-of-pv-antireflective-coatings-by-robot-cleaning/) (attribution of the quoted result between the PI Berlin and QEERI studies is uncertain from the excerpts).
- JinkoSolar "presented their Dust Cleaning Robot Testing Project; the company says it is the first module supplier to conduct such abrasion tests", results due late 2022 — reported in [Solar Quarter, Dec 2022](https://solarquarter.com/2022/12/26/top-factors-to-consider-before-choosing-a-safe-robotic-cleaning-solution/) and [pv magazine India, Nov 2022](https://pv-magazine-india.com/2022/11/09/key-factors-when-choosing-an-automated-cleaning-system-for-utility-scale-pv) (exact article attribution uncertain).
- Fraunhofer publication on robot cleaning effects exists — [Fraunhofer Publica](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/506717) (content not retrieved).
- DEWA (Dubai Electricity & Water Authority) study "assesses PV cleaning robot performance and safety" (Aug 14 2026; headline only) — [pv magazine, Aug 14 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/14/dewa-study-assesses-pv-cleaning-robot-performance-and-safety/).

- Module-OEM certification precedent: First Solar certified Ecoppia (2016) — [EnergyTrend](https://www.energytrend.com/news/20160105-10011.html).
- Tracker-OEM letter of compatibility: Nextracker confirmed KP Group's KPI Green OMS robot "meets its testing criteria" (Apr 2025) — [UNI India](https://test.uniindia.com/kp-group-39-s-solar-panel-cleaning-robot-receives-approval-from-nextracker/pnn/news/3434276.html); GameChange Genius Tracker 1P "designed to seamlessly integrate with leading solar module robotic cleaning solutions" (Jun 2024) — [GameChange Solar](https://www.gamechangesolar.com/news/gamechange-solar-announces-seamless-integration-of-genius-tracker-with-robotic-cleaners); [Solar Industry](https://solarindustrymag.com/gamechange-solar-integrates-genius-tracker-with-robotic-cleaners/embed).
- Coating validation precedents: DSM used TÜV SÜD's Dunhuang desert site — [Power Info Today](https://www.powerinfotoday.com/solar-energy/dsm-introduces-high-performance-anti-soiling-coating-for-solar-cover-glass-same-sun-more-power/); NREL 5-year multi-site glass-coating study — [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1458821); Sol Clarity ran a utility-hosted pilot (Nexamp) — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2024/07/11/solar-panel-cleaning-with-electromagnetic-waves/).
- Buyer checklists: "Key factors when choosing an automated cleaning system for utility scale PV" and "Top factors to consider before choosing a safe robotic cleaning solution" — [pv magazine India](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/?p=5838); [Solar Quarter](https://solarquarter.com/2022/12/26/top-factors-to-consider-before-choosing-a-safe-robotic-cleaning-solution/).

### Inferences
- The industry has converged on a two-layer validation: (1) lab abrasion per IEC 62788-7-3 on the specific module glass/coating; (2) module-OEM (and tracker-OEM) written compatibility. Independent-engineer (DNV/TÜV) sign-off appears to ride on these rather than on separate robot certifications.
- Because QEERI found that abrasion sensitivity is module-specific, buyers will want evidence for their exact module BOM; a new entrant should budget for per-OEM test campaigns (Jinko, LONGi, Trina, JA, First Solar) rather than one generic certificate.
- For coatings, the hard question is abrasion durability under the buyer's cleaning regime (NREL found dry-brush field acceleration factor ≈1, i.e., lab brushing approximates field wear), so a self-cleaning coating vendor must show compatibility with the robots the plant already uses.

- The contents of the PI Berlin, DEWA and Fraunhofer studies (damage magnitudes, methods) were not retrievable.
- No evidence found on how IEC 61215 (module qualification) is invoked in cleaning-related warranty disputes.

## Key Question 8: Transferable lessons for a new entrant selling self-cleaning technology

### Takeaway
The evidence suggests a new entrant should (a) secure one home-market showcase site with a sponsor motivated by innovation rather than price, (b) obtain IEC 62788-7-3-based abrasion data plus a module-OEM compatibility letter before approaching IPPs, (c) target one anchor developer and convert it to a master supply agreement, (d) secure tracker-OEM compatibility or partnership early, and (e) avoid competing head-on with Chinese robot pricing in India—position instead on yield guarantees, water-free coatings/EDS compatibility with existing robot fleets, or OEM bundling.

### Cited Findings
- Showcase-first: Ecoppia's Ketura Sun pilot → full site in <3 months → "world's first" PR — [PR Newswire 2014](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ecoppia-announces-worlds-first-completely-autonomously-cleaned-solar-energy-park-251939791.html).

### Inferences
- For a self-cleaning coating/EDS vendor the sharpest wedge is "robot-compatible or robot-reducing": IPPs now own thousands of robots, so a coating must prove it survives their brush regime (IEC 62788-7-3) and reduces cleaning frequency enough to be paid out of OPEX savings; this suggests selling through the robot vendors/O&M firms rather than against them.
- Because developer champions (Adani, Waaree, BluPine, Tata, ACWA) have already standardised on robots, the untapped entry points are (i) module OEM factory integration (DSM/Covestro route), (ii) tracker OEM bundling (Nextracker route), and (iii) markets where robots fit poorly (rooftop C&I, agrivoltaic/PM-KUSUM, floating PV, CSP mirrors—e.g., Nanoveu on heliostats).
- An IPO-first capital strategy is unlikely to repeat; strategic investors with a site to offer (utilities, oil & gas, construction/EPC) have provided both capital and first customers in every success case found.

### Gaps
- No case found of a performance-guaranteed (yield-linked) cleaning contract, so the viability of that model is untested in public sources.
- No evidence on how independent engineers (DNV, Black & Veatch) treat self-cleaning coatings in bankability reports.
