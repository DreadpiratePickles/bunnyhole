## Key Question 1: Typical and worst-case annual soiling energy losses by region

### Takeaway
The best-cited global figures are IEA-PVPS/Fraunhofer (Ilse et al.): 3-4% of global PV output lost in 2018 (>= EUR 3-5 bn revenue), rising to 4-7% (>= EUR 4-7 bn) by 2023 even with optimized cleaning; regional worst cases are far higher (Atacama north coast up to 39%/yr; Gulf 0.1-0.9%/day; India 15-35% after one uncleaned month per vendors), while rainy regions lose <1%.

### Cited Findings
**Global**
- IEA-PVPS Task 13/Task 16 fact sheet "Understanding, Measuring, and Mitigating Soiling Losses in PV Power Systems" (Sept 2025) estimates an average global energy loss of 4-7% from soiling, translating to multi-billion-euro annual revenue losses; it includes a per-country revenue-loss map built on Ilse et al. (2019) — [IEA-PVPS fact sheet page](https://iea-pvps.org/fact-sheets/fs-soiling-losses/); [PDF](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Fact-Sheet-Task-1316-Soiling.pdf); DLR mirror of the same fact sheet describes soiling as "an average, growing, 4% to 7% global loss" — [DLR elib](https://elib.dlr.de/218587/1/Fact-Sheet-Task-1316-Soiling.pdf)
- The earlier IEA-PVPS Task 13 report "Soiling Losses – Impact on the Performance of Photovoltaic Power Plants" (2022) estimated a 3-5% annual energy loss, equivalent to roughly EUR 3-5 billion in economic loss from higher operating, cleaning or capital costs — [IEA-PVPS key topic page](https://iea-pvps.org/key-topics/soiling-losses-impact-on-the-performance-of-photovoltaic-power-plants/)
- Trade press coverage of the Sept 2025 fact sheet: "soiling costs solar industry billions per year" — [pv magazine, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/10/01/iea-pvps-warns-soiling-costs-solar-industry-billions-per-year/); "Solar Power Output Drops 4-7% Globally Due to Soiling" — [Saur Energy, 2025](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/solar-power-output-drops-47-globally-due-to-soiling-pvps-warns-10524056). Note: pv magazine Australia's version says "billions of dollars" while the IEA source uses euros — [pv magazine Australia, 2 Oct 2025](https://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/2025/10/02/iea-pvps-warns-soiling-costs-solar-industry-billions-per-year/)
- Primary peer-reviewed source: Ilse et al., "Techno-Economic Assessment of Soiling Losses and Mitigation Strategies for Solar Power Generation," Joule (2019): soiling reduced global solar power production by at least 3-4% in 2018, causing global revenue losses of at least EUR 3-5 billion; modeled the 20 top PV markets (~90% of 2018 installed capacity) plus the global CSP market; projected that even with optimized cleaning, losses rise to 4-7% and >EUR 4-7 billion in 2023 — [Joule full text](https://www.cell.com/joule/fulltext/S2542-4351(19)30422-2); [Fraunhofer Publica entry](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/entities/publication/6a30e237-67ef-4994-aa45-307268530380); [DLR PDF mirror](https://elib.dlr.de/129424/1/Joule-ils.pdf)
- Older/less verifiable: Wikipedia cites an NREL-attributed global average of 5-10% annual power loss "as of 2018" and notes losses can be below 1% in regions with frequent rain (the underlying NREL document could not be verified) — [Wikipedia: Soiling (solar energy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soiling_(solar_energy))
- kWh Analytics 2024 Solar Risk Assessment (6th edition) focused on soiling, citing research that unmitigated soiling can cause energy production losses of up to 50% in specific regions — [SolarAnywhere summary of 2024 SRA](https://www.solaranywhere.com/2024/2024-solar-risk-assessment-report-data-driven-insights-address-emerging-risks/)

**Middle East / Gulf**
- DEWA study (published in Solar Energy; reported Aug 2026): at a desert test facility beside the Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Solar Park, baseline soiling rates were roughly 0.14-0.33% per day; daily robotic cleaning reduced soiling rates to below 0.02%/day, and robots achieved cleaning efficiencies of 69-99% — [pv magazine, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/08/14/dewa-study-assesses-pv-cleaning-robot-performance-and-safety/)
- Saudi Arabia: Rumah sites showed seasonal soiling rates of ~0.1-0.57%/day; a Dhahran study found 0.28%/day; a broader Saudi compilation reported an average of 0.051%/day with 26% of sites above 0.1%/day — [ResearchGate: The Impact of Soiling on PV Module Performance in Saudi Arabia (2022)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364844999_The_Impact_of_Soiling_on_PV_Module_Performance_in_Saudi_Arabia)
- UAE (Abu Dhabi): one study found 0.87%/day in March, the worst month — same source as above, [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364844999_The_Impact_of_Soiling_on_PV_Module_Performance_in_Saudi_Arabia)
- Older (2016) central Saudi cleaning optimization study exists: "Optimized Cleaning Cost and Schedule Based on Observed Soiling Conditions for Photovoltaic Plants in Central Saudi Arabia" — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/297892023_Optimized_Cleaning_Cost_and_Schedule_Based_on_Observed_Soiling_Conditions_for_Photovoltaic_Plants_in_Central_Saudi_Arabia)
- FLAG (marketing/SEO, implausible): a Saudi consultancy page claims Saudi soiling losses "run around 2 to 5 percent per day," an order of magnitude above peer-reviewed values — [Saudi Energy Consulting](https://saudienergyconsulting.com/insights/articles/winning-the-desert-battle-solar-panel-soiling-in-saudi-arabia-and-how-to-fight-it)
- Qatar: a 19-month field validation of the DustIQ optical soiling sensor at a Qatar test facility found good agreement with the conventional clean/soiled reference-module method — [IEEE Xplore (2024)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10749590/)

**Chile (Atacama)**
- A year-long measurement campaign across five Atacama cities found deposition plus infrequent rain produced annual energy losses peaking at 39% in the northern coastal desert versus ~7% in the wetter central zone; a secondhand citation gives ~0.6%/day in the north — [ResearchGate: Quantifying PV soiling losses in the Atacama Desert (2021)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354663174_Quantifying_PV_soiling_losses_in_the_Atacama_Desert); earlier study: [Effects of soiling on PV modules in the Atacama Desert (PMC, 2018)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6141476/)
- A newer study of "cemented" soiling reports annual energy losses of up to 9.8% if not addressed — reported via [ResearchGate search results, Atacama coastal study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343190542_Determination_of_the_Soiling_Impact_on_Photovoltaic_Modules_at_the_Coastal_Area_of_the_Atacama_Desert)
- Microbial biofilm soiling (lab, accelerated): seven days of colonization produced short-circuit-current losses of 15.20-30.66% in samples from the University of Antofagasta — [pv magazine, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/04/16/microbial-biofilms-can-reduce-solar-module-yield-by-up-to-30-in-arid-zones/); [Olivares et al., Advanced Sustainable Systems 2026](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adsu.202501181); [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/technology/microbial-soiling-adds-new-layer-to-pv-performance-losses)
- pv magazine ran a Dec 2025 feature on how the Atacama region handles soiling and cleaning (content not retrievable) — [pv magazine, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/12/09/how-the-worlds-sunniest-region-tackles-solar-module-soiling-cleaning/)

**Australia**
- Applied Energy (2022) multi-dataset study: most operational Australian solar farms showed energy reductions of 8-9% from dust that regular seasonal rain then removed; sites without natural removal showed losses up to 3% around the Lake Eyre Basin — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S030626192200099X); [ADS abstract](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApEn..31018626P/abstract)
- Murdoch University thesis: one-year dust-induced power loss of 4-6% in Perth vs 16-18% in Nusa Tenggara Timur, Indonesia; the AS/NZS 4509.2:2010 standard's 5% dust derating suited Perth modelling provided panels were cleaned about once per year — [Murdoch University](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/doctoral/The-effect-of-dust-on-the/991005542664907891)

**United States**
- NREL "Availability and Performance Loss Factors for U.S. PV Fleet Systems" (Feb 2024): fleet data from >2,200 systems; soiling values (CODS method in RdTools) presented for 255 systems that showed irradiance-weighted soiling loss >1%, published in an updated NREL soiling map; median system degradation -0.5% to -0.75%/yr (degradation, not soiling) — [NREL PDF](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy24osti/88769.pdf); [OSTI record](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2318550)
- The original NREL soiling map (Micheli, Deceglie, Muller) used 83 US sites; models and data agree the highest soiling losses are in the Southwest — [OSTI: Tracking Soiling Losses: Assessment, Uncertainty and Challenges in Mapping](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1832410); [NREL: Mapping Photovoltaic Soiling Using Spatial Interpolation](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1479873); [NREL 2023 on uncertainty of soiling estimation](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy23osti/81014.pdf)
- NREL single-system case (2020): estimated average soiling loss 2.2% (95% CI 1.5-3.2%) alongside -0.6%/yr degradation — [NREL PDF](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy20osti/76066.pdf)
- NREL program overview "PV Soiling Losses: Measurements, Modeling, and Mitigation Strategies" (2022) — [NREL PDF](https://docs.nrel.gov/docs/fy22osti/83486.pdf) (not readable in this session)

**India**
- (vendor) Indian average soiling rate cited as ~0.5%/day; 15-35% loss after ~30 days without cleaning in high-dust states (Gujarat, Rajasthan), some attributed to CEEW but not verifiable — [Heaven Green Energy (vendor)](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/solar-cleaning-frequency-india); [Taypro June 2026 research page (vendor)](https://taypro.in/insights/solar-cleaning-research-june-2026)
- (vendor) 5-25% losses reported in reservoir/floating and utility settings — [Taypro floating-solar blog (vendor)](https://taypro.in/blog/floating-solar-panel-cleaning-challenges-for-indian-reservoir-projects)

**Plant-type effects (bifacial, tilt)**
- Rear-side soiling on bifacial minimodules measured at ~0.039%/day, ~8.8x lower than front side; a Chile study found rear-side soiling ~9x lower than front; bifacial modules overall less soiling-sensitive than monofacial — [ResearchGate: Effect of soiling in bifacial PV modules and cleaning schedule optimization (2018)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327363934_Effect_of_soiling_in_bifacial_PV_modules_and_cleaning_schedule_optimization)
- Modeling: soiling loss decreases with increasing tilt angle, so higher-tilt arrays (and trackers at high tilt) resist soiling better — [arXiv 1709.10026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.10026)
- Kipp & Zonen notes the bottom edge of modules often soils more than the top — [Kipp & Zonen DustIQ](https://www.kippzonen.com/Product/419/DustIQ-Soiling-Monitoring-System)

**Soiling as P50 uncertainty**
- kWh Analytics 2022 Solar Generation Index: soiling loss inputs in energy models contribute up to 4% of P50 uncertainty; 2021 SRA found independent-engineer soiling estimates were badly off versus on-site soiling measurements — [SGI 2022 PDF](https://kwhanalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/SGI-2022.FINAL_.pdf); [SRA 2021 PDF](https://kwhanalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kWhAnalytics_SolarRiskAssessment21_06-08-21.pdf)

### Inferences
- A seller can safely quote "4-7% of global PV output, multi-billion euros/year" (IEA-PVPS 2025) as the headline; "3-5%" (IEA-PVPS 2022) and "5-10%" (older) exist, so cite the year with every figure.
- Daily rates of 0.1-0.9%/day in the Gulf and ~0.6%/day in northern Chile mean a single uncleaned month costs 3-25% of output; these sites are the economic core of the self-cleaning value proposition.
- Bifacial rear-side soiling is a second-order issue; front-side soiling on high-tilt trackers is lower than fixed low-tilt, so the sharpest pitch is to low-tilt fixed, desert, and agricultural-dust sites.

### Gaps
- No NREL fleet-median soiling %/yr figure for the US was retrievable (requires the full FY24 report or interactive map); no regional %/yr values for California Central Valley or Arizona were obtained before the search budget expired.
- No peer-reviewed India-wide soiling rate was found; all India figures are vendor claims.
- Qatar, Kuwait, Egypt, Morocco daily rates were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- No Australian utility-scale %/day rate exists in the literature found.

## Key Question 2: Global solar O&M market size and panel-cleaning segment size/forecast

### Takeaway
Wood Mackenzie counts 348 GW under third-party O&M at end-2025 (+61 GW YoY, top-15 providers 57%) with North American full-wrap prices down 18% YoY; dollar sizing of the cleaning segment is only available from market-research firms, which cluster at roughly USD 1.2-1.5 bn (2024/25) growing to USD 2.3-2.5 bn by 2030/31 (~8-9% CAGR), while robot-only estimates diverge five-fold (USD 0.16-1.2 bn in 2024/25).

## Key Question 3: What asset owners pay (manual vs robotic vs waterless), frequency, and labor/water/breakage drivers

### Takeaway
Per-pass manual cleaning prices cluster at roughly USD 0.4-1.75/panel in the US (USD ~1,000-7,000/MW/pass, scale-dependent) and INR 0.45-1.2/module in India (roughly INR 1,350-4,200/MW/pass), with robotic/waterless cleaning quoted at INR 0.20/module ("six times cheaper") and USD ~2,400-4,000/MW/yr; vendors claim 1.5-6 year paybacks, robots at 1-2% of site capex, and water savings of 5,000-24,000 L per MW per wash.

### Cited Findings

## Key Question 4: Who owns the cleaning/O&M budget and which KPIs they report

### Takeaway
The budget sits with the asset owner/IPP's asset-management function but is executed by O&M contractors (increasingly consolidated: top-15 providers hold 57% of 348 GW), and the KPIs that drive cleaning decisions are performance ratio (PR, typically 75-85%), energy-based availability (contractual 98%+), soiling ratio (measured PR / clean PR, cleaning trigger ~0.97) and OPEX per MW or per MWh reported to lenders alongside DSCR.

### Cited Findings
- IEA-PVPS Task 13 "Guidelines for Operation and Maintenance of PV Power Plants" (T13-25, 2022): guaranteed PR and guaranteed plant availability are the core contractual KPIs; after guaranteed PR, guaranteed plant availability is the most commonly used basis for O&M guarantees — [IEA-PVPS O&M Guidelines PDF](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/IEA-PVPS-Report-T13-25-2022-OandM-Guidelines.pdf)
- Greensolver (asset-management consultancy): recommends energy-based availability with a minimum guaranteed availability of 98% per operational year; notes asset owners have been moving away from PR guarantees toward response-time guarantees — [Greensolver](https://greensolver.net/articles/key-solar-om-contract-elements-asset-owners-should-adopt-to-improve-their-overall-asset-performance/); [Greensolver blog](https://blog.greensolver.net/en/key-solar-om-contract-elements-asset-owners-should-adopt-to-improve-their-over-all-asset-performance/)
- SolarPower Europe "Solar Best Practices": PR warranties are no longer standard in the independent/third-party O&M market; during the warranty period the asset manager monitors, calculates, reports and follows up PR and other KPIs guaranteed by the EPC contractor — [Solar Best Practices: contractual framework](https://solarbestpractices.com/guidelines/detail/contractual-framework); [Solar Best Practices: asset management](https://solarbestpractices.com/guidelines/detail/asset-management)
- IRENA Open Solar Contracts O&M Agreement draft: 98% bracketed minimum availability; the O&M contractor is typically not required to guarantee PR but should monitor and report it; the finance agreement lets the lender's independent consultant weigh in on certain contract terms — [IRENA Open Solar Contracts O&M draft](https://www.opensolarcontracts.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/OpenSolarContracts/Contracts/OM-Agreement-Review-Draft.pdf?la=en&hash=66303CBB982D2843CAAF2C50C37BB797C26646B7)
- Sandia/NREL best-practice paper: PR should be assessed under a separate performance guarantee rather than inside the availability guarantee, because PR is affected by weather and derate uncertainty such as soiling — [OSTI: A Best Practice for Developing Availability Guarantee Language](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1227340); companion report on O&M budgeting practices and pricing — [OSTI: Budgeting for Solar PV Plant O&M](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1234935)
- Operator KPI guides: a well-performing utility-scale plant typically achieves PR of 80-85% after temperature correction; PR below 75% usually indicates soiling, inverter downtime, shading or degradation — [Promeraki](https://promeraki.com/blog/solar-pv-plant-kpis); 75-85% for a well-maintained plant — [Clearspot](https://clearspot.ai/solar-portfolio-monitoring-utility-scale-operator-guide-2026/)
- Soiling ratio defined as measured PR divided by clean PR, with a cleaning trigger below 0.97; cleaning should be scheduled on measured soiling ratio rather than the calendar — [Blu Banyan KPI list](https://blubanyan.com/solar-asset-performance-monitoring-kpi-list/)
- Asset-management software guides list DSCR and LLCR monitoring, budget variance by OPEX category, and lender reporting automation as IPP asset-manager duties; OPEX per MWh vs OPEX per Wp both used — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-asset-management-guide); [Stakelum Consultancy](https://www.stakelumconsultancy.com/post/renewable-energy-kpis); C&I financing KPIs — [Odyssey Energy Solutions](https://odysseyenergysolutions.com/news-insights/the-c-i-solar-financing-kpis-that-matter-the-most)
- pv magazine on operator KPIs and the "data deluge" (2021) — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2021/09/27/kpis-solar-operators-should-be-tracking-to-overcome-the-data-deluge/)
- O&M contract types (full-wrap vs scope-based) explained — [Heaven Green glossary](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/glossary/o-and-m-contract-types)
- Budget concentration: top 15 O&M providers manage ~200 GW (57%) of 348 GW; NovaSource 38.4 GW — [Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/ja/press-releases/global-solar-pv-om-market-reaches-348-gw-as-consolidation-tightens-and-costs-fall/)
- Robotic cleaning is specified at design/EPC stage in large Gulf projects (e.g. ACWA's Sudair spec lists "an automated robotic cleaning solution") — [ACWA Power Sudair project page](https://acwapower.com/en/what-we-do/projects/sudair-pv-ipp/)
- Ecoppia (vendor): robots are 1-2% of site capex, i.e. a developer/EPC capex decision — [Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-news/robotic-cleaning-high-performance-solar-panels-will-be-vital-to-indian-solar-market-with-tracking-mounting-sys-amit-singla-ecoppia-india)

### Inferences
- Two distinct buyers: (a) developer/EPC at design stage for greenfield (capex line, 1-2% of capex, specified in Gulf tenders and large Indian IPP orders), and (b) asset owner/asset manager plus O&M contractor for operating plants (OPEX line). A self-cleaning technology that is integrated into the module or glass sells into (a); a retrofit sells into (b).
- Because PR guarantees are retreating from O&M contracts, O&M contractors have weaker contractual incentive to over-clean; the owner's asset manager (who answers to lenders on DSCR) is the party that captures soiling-recovery value and should be the primary economic buyer.
- A seller's ROI model should output exactly the KPIs owners report: delta PR (points), delta soiling ratio, availability (unchanged, so emphasise PR), OPEX/MW/yr saved, litres/MW/yr saved, and resulting DSCR uplift.

### Gaps
- No survey of what share of IPPs self-perform cleaning vs subcontract; no data on how cleaning is priced inside full-wrap contracts (per-pass pass-through vs fixed).
- No published figures on org-chart ownership (e.g., "head of asset management" vs "O&M manager") beyond generic guides.

## Key Question 5: How cleaning interacts with O&M contracts, PPAs, performance guarantees, IE reports and lenders

### Takeaway
Soiling enters the financing chain through the energy-yield model (IEs typically assume 1-3% flat soiling; PVsyst default 2%; 3-5% for conservative/P95 cases) and through PPA/O&M guarantees (PPA mechanical availability 90-95%; O&M availability 97-99%+ with liquidated damages), but availability guarantees do not capture soiling ("99% availability and 72% PR" is possible), and kWh Analytics shows US fleets running 8.6% below P50 in 2025 with optimistic P50s (including soiling inputs) the largest driver.

### Cited Findings
- IEA-PVPS T13-08 "Technical Assumptions Used in PV Financial Models" (2017, dated): expected soiling loss "is often provided and ranges from 1.0% to 3.0%" — [IEA-PVPS PDF](https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Report_IEA-PVPS_T13-08_2017_Technical_Assumptions_Used_in_PV_Financial_Models.pdf)
- PVsyst defaults to 2% soiling loss; a typical P50 soiling assumption is 2%, with 3-5% (site-specific) for a conservative P95 case; a 2% assumption in a dusty industrial zone would overstate output, reduce P90 and trigger lender haircuts — [Heaven Designs bankable PVsyst guide](https://heavendesigns.in/blog/bankable-pvsyst-reports-guide/); [Heaven Designs P50/P90 guide](https://heavendesigns.in/blog/p50-p90-solar-yield-reports/)
- An IE would flag an arbitrary 1% flat soiling loss at a dusty site without site data or a cleaning schedule tied to the O&M contract; lenders expect each loss line item (irradiance, IAV, model, soiling, degradation) to be justified with a reference or measurement — [SurgePV IE guide](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-independent-engineer); [Illuminei: What lenders flag](https://www.illuminei.com/blog/what-lenders-actually-flag-in-a-technical-package-a-guide-to-independent-engineering); [SgurrEnergy bankable EYA](https://sgurrenergy.com/resources/methodologies/bankable-energy-yield-assessment); [PlantPredict bankability](https://www.plantpredict.com/bankability)
- kWh Analytics 2025 Solar Risk Assessment: nationwide (US) generation 8.6% below P50 — [SRA 2025 PDF](https://kwhanalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Solar-Risk-Assessment-2025.pdf); 2024 SRA: overly optimistic P50s remain the largest driver of underperformance; unmitigated soiling up to 50% in specific regions — [SolarAnywhere summary](https://www.solaranywhere.com/2024/2024-solar-risk-assessment-report-data-driven-insights-address-emerging-risks/); 2022 SGI: P50 underperformance 7-13% for projects built after 2015; soiling inputs up to 4% of P50 uncertainty — [SGI 2022](https://kwhanalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/SGI-2022.FINAL_.pdf); [Solar Builder coverage](https://solarbuildermag.com/operations-maintenance/kwh-analytics-solar-assets-continued-to-underperform-p50-estimates/); [pv magazine 2022](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2022/12/07/us-solar-assets-underperforming-says-kwh-analytics/)
- Utility-scale PPAs: mechanical-availability guarantees usually 90-95% (sometimes lower in the first measurement period), measured after excluding force majeure, curtailment and some scheduled maintenance; availability damages are a set amount per percentage point of shortfall, paid or credited against buyer payments; sellers seek annual/aggregate LD caps — [Stoel Rives, The Law of Solar: PPAs](https://www.stoel.com/insights/reports/the-law-of-solar/power-purchase-agreements-utility-scale-projects); [Lex Helius PPA chapter PDF](https://www.agmrc.org/media/cms/Power_Purchase_Agreements__UtilityS_2F7F48DE81BC7.pdf)
- O&M availability guarantees typically 97-99%/yr; O&M contractor LDs are meant to compensate the IPP for exact PPA revenue lost to downtime — [Steve Hanawalt, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/10-changes-solar-asset-management-prepare-now-steve-hanawalt-1c); best practice "minimum guaranteed contractual availability of 99%+ over a year at inverter level" and module washing scope — [Caderco](https://www.caderco.co.za/articles/o-and-m-agreements-solar-wind-availability-guarantees); 95-98% with LDs below threshold — [Areg AI PPA guide](https://areg.ai/blog/the-solar-ppa-guide-for-asset-owners-and-investors/)
- "A plant can have 99% availability and 72% PR because it is always on but heavily soiled"; cleaning frequency should be set on data, not calendar; scheduled cleaning is standard in full-service O&M; a cleaning obligation should be tied to the production guarantee rather than availability — [Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/facility-management/solar-pv-system-maintenance-commercial-buildings)
- No standard calculation method exists for plant availability, so definitions matter — [Solar Best Practices](https://solarbestpractices.com/guidelines/detail/contractual-framework)
- IRENA draft: lender's independent consultant can weigh in on O&M terms — [IRENA Open Solar Contracts](https://www.opensolarcontracts.org/-/media/Files/IRENA/OpenSolarContracts/Contracts/OM-Agreement-Review-Draft.pdf?la=en&hash=66303CBB982D2843CAAF2C50C37BB797C26646B7)

### Inferences
- The highest-leverage bankability pitch is at the IE/financial-model stage: if a self-cleaning technology lets the IE reduce the soiling line from 2-5% to ~1% with documented evidence, it lifts P50/P90 directly, which is worth far more to a developer than OPEX savings (every 1% of yield on a 100 MW plant at ~2,000 MWh/MW/yr and USD 30-50/MWh is USD 60-100k/yr).
- Because availability guarantees exclude soiling, a self-cleaning vendor should not expect pull from O&M contractors' LD exposure; instead target owners carrying PPA production guarantees and lenders' DSCR covenants.
- IEs will require measured data (DustIQ/MARS or clean-reference modules) to accept a lower soiling assumption; bundling monitoring with the technology strengthens bankability.

### Gaps
- No published example of a lender or IE formally crediting a cleaning technology with a reduced soiling assumption was found.
- No data on how often PPAs carry output (as opposed to availability) guarantees that expose owners to soiling.
- The full IEA-PVPS 2022 O&M guideline and 2025 soiling report sections on contractual treatment of cleaning could not be read.

### Inferences
- Robot incumbency is highest exactly where soiling is worst (India, Gulf): a self-cleaning technology must displace or complement robots there, so the pitch needs a robot-vs-self-cleaning comparison (capex 1-2% of site, 1.5-6 yr payback claims, row-end hardware, breakage risk).
- Chile, US Southwest and Australia's interior have high soiling but low robot penetration and higher labor costs, suggesting better greenfield receptivity for a non-robot approach; Australia's rain-cleaning reduces the annual-value case outside the Lake Eyre interior.
- Low-tilt fixed arrays (and the lower edge of modules) soil most; bifacial rear sides matter little; floating solar is a niche with special access problems.
- Module-glass-integrated self-cleaning is likely to be sold to module OEMs/EPCs (design stage), while retrofit coatings/systems sell to owners of fleets built before ~2020.

### Gaps
- No market-wide share of utility-scale plants using robotic cleaning in India, the Gulf or globally; only vendor-level claims (Sol-Bright 40 GW cumulative worldwide, LEAPTING 10 GW India).
- No data on robot penetration in the US, Chile or Australia.
- Floating solar and C&I cleaning market sizes not found.

## Key Question 7: Recent (2024-2026) trends that matter

### Takeaway
Five trends line up for a self-cleaning seller: (1) OPEX is a growing share of LCOE as capex/module prices bottom (modules ~USD 0.09/Wdc in Q1 2025; US O&M medians down ~74% since 2011 yet OPEX cited at 20-30% of LCOE); (2) O&M price compression (-18% NA full-wrap) and skilled-labor shortages; (3) water scarcity driving waterless/robotic adoption (India MNRE advisory, Gulf waterless specs, DEWA validation); (4) rapid consolidation among Chinese robot makers (Sol-Bright, LEAPTING) and M&A (Ecoppia reportedly acquired by StarkPower, Jan 2026); (5) soiling measurement maturing (DustIQ/MARS field-validated; NREL fleet map at 255 systems; new microbial-soiling research).

### Inferences
- The combination of cheap modules, compressed O&M pricing and labor shortages makes "labor-free, water-free yield recovery" the right framing; the OPEX-share-of-LCOE argument is widely asserted but the exact percentage (20-30% vs 77%) is contested, so quote LBNL/NREL absolute numbers rather than a share.
- Chinese robot vendors are winning on price in India and the Gulf (Sol-Bright doubling share in one year), which implies the incumbent benchmark a self-cleaning technology must beat is a low-cost Chinese row robot, not a premium Israeli one.
- Ecoppia's reported sale (if confirmed) signals that pure-play robot economics have been difficult; this is useful competitive context but needs verification from the acquirer.
- Because IEs now expect site-specific soiling evidence, pairing the technology with DustIQ/MARS-class monitoring converts a marketing claim into bankable data.

- No market-adoption data for soiling monitoring stations (units or share of plants).
- The StarkPower acquisition of Ecoppia is listed only on PitchBook and could not be corroborated.
- No 2025-2026 regulatory texts (India, Gulf, US states) mandating water limits on PV cleaning were found; the MNRE instrument is a 2019 advisory.
