## Key Question 1: Latest capacity, annual additions, pipeline, and where soiling-prone utility-scale capacity sits

### Takeaway
India had ~171 GW of installed solar at end-September 2026 (ground-mounted ~126 GW), added a record 44.6 GW in FY2025-26 and 35.2 GW in Jan–Sep 2026, and carries a >200 GW large-scale pipeline; Rajasthan (~44 GW) and Gujarat (~35 GW) host the dusty, water-scarce mega-parks (Bhadla, Khavda) where cleaning technology has the clearest economic case.

### Cited Findings
**Installed base and additions**
- MNRE data put cumulative installed solar at 171.051 GW as of 30 Sep 2026; ground-mounted projects 125.65 GW and grid-connected rooftop 33.41 GW; India added 35.24 GW in Jan–Sep 2026 including 3.011 GW in September alone — [Sustainable Construction Now, Oct 2026](https://www.sustainableconstruction-now.com/article/556391/indias-solar-pv-capacity-crosses-171-gw-in-september-2026-as-installations-accelerate-nationwide); corroborated by [Anya Energy](https://anyaenergy.com/india-solar-capacity-171-gw-milestone-2026/)
- Cumulative solar surpassed 164 GW with ~28.78 GW added Jan–Jul 2026 (164.59 GW at end-July; ~2.44 GW added in July) — [SolarQuarter, 12 Aug 2026](https://solarquarter.com/2026/08/12/indias-solar-capacity-surpasses-164-gw-as-2026-installations-approach-29-gw-by-july-end/)
- FY2025-26 was the highest single-year solar addition ever at 44.61 GW (MNRE), more than double the FY2024-25 record; the base was 150.26 GW on 31 Mar 2026 — [pv magazine India, 10 Apr 2026](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/04/10/india-installs-record-45-gw-solar-capacity-in-fy2026/); JMK Research gives ~44.6 GW solar and ~6 GW wind for FY2026 — [JMK Research](https://jmkresearch.com/india-installs-record-44-gw-solar-and-6-gw-wind-capacity-in-fy2026/)
- H1 2026: Mercom counts 27 GW of solar installed (up 49% from 18 GW in H1 2025) and cumulative 165 GW at end-June 2026 — [TaiyangNews citing Mercom](https://taiyangnews.info/markets/mercom-india-solar-installations-h1-2026); JMK counts 26 GW solar + 3 GW wind in H1 2026, with Gujarat (7.6 GW) and Rajasthan (6.6 GW) leading solar installations — [JMK Research H1 2026](https://jmkresearch.com/india-installs-record-26-gw-solar-and-3-gw-wind-capacity-in-h12026/)
- Nine-month 2026 additions were ~35.2 GW, up 19.5% YoY (JMK) — [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/india-adds-35-2gw-of-solar-capacity-in-nine-months-report/)
- India ranks third globally in renewable installed capacity (government statement) — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2250039&lang=1&reg=3); [PV Tech on third-largest solar PV capacity](https://www.pv-tech.org/india-becomes-third-largest-country-for-solar-pv-capacity/)

**Targets and pipeline**
- Mercom's tracker put India's large-scale solar pipeline at 208 GW at end-June 2026 (all stages: tendered, auctioned, under construction, awaiting commissioning) — [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/indias-large-scale-solar-pipeline-tops-200-gw); summarized by [Ornate Solar](https://ornatesolar.com/news/indias-solar-project-pipeline-exceeds-200-gw-bringing-2030-target-within-reach)

- Two 2030 targets are in use: 280 GW of installed solar (Mercom-cited framing) and 500 GW of non-fossil capacity; MNRE reported cumulative non-fossil capacity of 300.5 GW as of 31 Jul 2026, over 60% of the 500 GW goal — [Ornate Solar summarizing Mercom/MNRE](https://ornatesolar.com/news/indias-solar-project-pipeline-exceeds-200-gw-bringing-2030-target-within-reach)
- India's floating solar pipeline exceeds 1 GW — [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/indias-floating-solar-pipeline-tops-1-gw-but-vast-potential-remains-untapped)

**State ranking (MNRE state-wise data, secondary sources)**
- As of 31 Aug 2026: Rajasthan ~44.4 GW (1st), Gujarat ~34.9 GW (2nd), then Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu, with Karnataka ~12.0 GW (5th); national total ~168 GW on that date — [Heaven Green Energy blog citing MNRE](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/top-solar-energy-producing-states-in-india); a Wikipedia table of MNRE data as of 31 Jul 2026 gives Rajasthan ~44.1 GW and Gujarat ~33.8 GW — [Wikipedia: States of India by installed power capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_India_by_installed_power_capacity); state-wise RE analysis as of 30 Apr 2026 — [Eninrac](https://eninrac.com/horizon/insights/state-wise-renewable-energy-installed-capacity-analysis-as-of-30-april-2026/)

**The mega-parks where soiling is severe**
- Khavda (Kutch, Gujarat): Adani Green reports 9.4 GW operational at 31 Mar 2026, 5.1 GW commissioned in FY2025-26, on track for the full 30 GW by 2029 (20 GW solar + 10 GW wind over 538 sq km) — [Adani Green Energy Khavda digital report 2026](https://www.adanigreenenergy.com/digital-reports/green-energy/2026/khavda-re-project.html); secondary reports put operational capacity near 10 GW by mid-2026 — [Blackridge Research project profile](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/project-profiles/worlds-largest-renewable-energy-park-khavda-hybrid-solar-wind-kutch-gujarat-india); Adani added 150 MW in May 2026 — [pv magazine, 11 May 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/11/adani-adds-150-mw-solar-capacity-to-khavda-30-gw-renewable-energy-park/); Khavda BESS expanded to 6.63 GWh on 1 Oct 2026 (from 3.55 GWh in June) — [Wins Solutions (secondary)](https://www.winssolutions.org/adani-green-khavda-battery-storage-6-63-gwh/); NTPC Green has commissioned phases at Khavda-II including 270 MW — [Blackridge Research](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/project-profiles/worlds-largest-renewable-energy-park-khavda-hybrid-solar-wind-kutch-gujarat-india)
- At Khavda "frequent sandstorms necessitate cleaning of solar panels several times in a day", and AGEL "has committed to deploy waterless cleaning robots for the entire solar capacity to address dust accumulation"; AGEL's operating portfolio is certified "water positive for plants of more than 200 MW capacity" — [AGEL press release, Mar 2024](https://www.adanigreenenergy.com/newsroom/media-releases/adani-green-energy-operationalizes-1000-mw-of-the-30000-mw-khavda-renewable-energy-park); [Business Standard, Mar 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/adani-green-operationlises-cumulative-1-000-mw-solar-projects-at-khavda-124031100198_1.html)
- Bhadla (Jodhpur, Rajasthan) is consistently listed as India's largest operational park at 2,245 MW; Pavagada (Karnataka) 2,050 MW (some lists say 2,000 MW); Kurnool/Ananthapuramu (AP) listed at 1,000–2,000 MW depending on source; Rewa (MP) 750 MW; Kamuthi (TN) 648 MW — [KLK India 2026 list](https://www.klkindia.com/2026/04/03); [Blackridge top-10 list](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/blog/list-of-top-largest-solar-plants-farms-parks-in-india); [Infralens Pavagada profile](https://infralens.in/projects/pavagada-solar-park)
- A field case study of Bhadla found developers draw cleaning water from neighboring canals, tubewells and ponds in an area that "lacks immediate access to water"; a local sarpanch acknowledged "acute water scarcity"; estimated water use is 7–8 litres per panel wash — [CENFA, Jan 2024 (PDF)](https://www.cenfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Report_Solar-Power-in-India-A-Case-Study-of-the-Bhadla-Solar-Power-Park.pdf)
- Fortum's Bhadla project is described as sitting in a "water-deficient region" that "often faces massive dust storms" — [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/ecoppia-fortum-solar-projects-pavagada-bhadla)
- A 2026 design guide cites Thar Desert dust loss of 10–25% without cleaning and advises deionised/RO water only in Rajasthan (technical recommendation, not regulation) — [Heaven Green Energy design guide](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/solar-in-rajasthan-dust-heat)

### Inferences
- Roughly three-quarters of India's solar fleet is ground-mounted (125.65 of 171 GW), and the two leading states (Rajasthan + Gujarat, ~79 GW) are both arid/desert regions, so the addressable utility-scale market for soiling solutions is concentrated in a handful of districts (Jodhpur/Jaisalmer/Bikaner/Barmer in Rajasthan; Kutch/Banaskantha in Gujarat) plus Pavagada/Tumkur in Karnataka.
- The 208 GW pipeline against ~45 GW/yr of additions implies the next 3–4 years of new builds are already visible in tender/auction records; a vendor can target projects at the EPC-design stage, which is when robotic cleaning is now being specified (see Q3).

### Gaps
- No primary MNRE/CEA monthly report was readable in this session (secondary sources only); the state-wise figures should be verified against MNRE's "Physical Progress" page.
- No breakdown of the 208 GW pipeline by stage (under construction vs. merely tendered) was found.
- No 2025-26 capacity figures were found for Bhadla or Pavagada beyond the long-standing 2,245 MW / 2,050 MW totals; whether Khavda has now passed Bhadla as the largest operating solar site (as opposed to hybrid park) is not confirmed by a primary source.

## Key Question 2: Current cost of panel cleaning in India, cleaning frequency, and documented soiling losses

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed Indian soiling rates cluster around 0.2–0.4%/day (up to ~0.55%/day in literature) with spring peaks; Indian O&M runs roughly ₹3.5–8 lakh/MW/yr of which cleaning labour and water is about ₹1–2 lakh/MW/yr, versus ~₹1.2–1.5 lakh/MW-DC/yr cited for robotic cleaning; manual wet cleaning uses ~1–8 litres per panel per wash in a water-scarce landscape — but no independent, current (2025-26) per-MW manual-cleaning cost or cleaner wage benchmark was found.

### Cited Findings
**Soiling rates and losses (academic/peer-reviewed)**
- De et al. (2025), "Estimation of non-uniform soiling loss in a utility-scale PV plant in India and strategies for enhanced performance through optimal cleaning schedules": estimates string-level soiling in an Indian utility plant, tests cleaning thresholds of 85%, 90% and 95%, reports per-string rates (e.g., about −0.159%/day) and finds strings soiling faster than 0.25%/day justify more frequent cleaning; search excerpts attribute a per-string manual cleaning cost of ₹72 and per-panel water-based cleaning of ₹50–90 to this paper (attribution of these cost figures not fully verified) — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390381313_Estimation_of_non-uniform_soiling_loss_in_a_utility-scale_PV_plant_in_India_and_strategies_for_enhanced_performance_through_optimal_cleaning_schedules)
- A rooftop BAPV study in India's composite climate reports the highest soiling rate in spring (~0.39%/day) and lowest in monsoon (~0.24%/day) — [ResearchGate (2022)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362135929_Assessment_of_soiling_impacts_and_cleaning_frequencies_of_a_rooftop_BAPV_system_in_composite_climates_of_India)
- A 2026 Scientific Reports (Nature) experimental study at Roorkee cites a general Indian range of 0.05–0.55%/day, measured daily averages of 0.17–0.21%/day, and found long cleaning intervals produced soiling losses above 5% — [Nature Scientific Reports, 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-45485-2)
- A rooftop study in composite climate on elevated temperature plus soiling loss (PMC, 2025) — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12658225/)
- An arXiv 2026 review of RL-based cleaning scheduling notes a Kuwait study needing weekly cleaning in high-soiling months and ~30-day intervals otherwise (non-India, for context) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07518)
- A global optimal-cleaning-cycle analysis (Applied Energy, 2021) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306261921000039)

**Soiling losses (industry/vendor claims; lower reliability)**
- Taypro (vendor) claims 30–40% annual loss with no cleaning at Barmer/Jodhpur and 4–7% with weekly cleaning; 0.3–0.5%/day in West India dry seasons; 8–25% seasonal losses on utility plants, attributed to "industry discussions" — [Taypro Rajasthan/Gujarat blog](https://taypro.in/blog/average-soiling-losses-in-high-dust-regions-of-india-rajasthan-gujarat); [Taypro soiling calculator](https://taypro.in/solar-panel-soiling-loss-calculator); [Taypro seasonal blog](https://taypro.in/blog/seasonal-variation-in-soiling-rates-and-energy-yield-loss-in-india)
- Heaven Green Energy (vendor) puts uncleaned losses at 15–35% in high-dust states — [Heaven Green glossary](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/glossary/soiling-loss)
- Renewable Watch (2020) cites up to 25% annual generation loss on poorly cleaned sites and says robotic cleaning reduces water need by 50–100% — [Renewable Watch, Dec 2020](https://renewablewatch.in/2020/12/03/waterless-way/)
- Miraikikai (Japan) said at its 2019 Bhadla trial that "many plants have implemented manual wet cleaning, which undermines profitability as the result of high water and labor cost" — [pv magazine press release](https://www.pv-magazine.com/press-releases/miraikikai-to-commence-dry-cleaning-robot-operation-at-bhadla-solar-park-rajasthan/)

**O&M and cleaning costs**
- Saur Energy (industry article, ~3 years old): average Indian solar O&M ~₹3.5 lakh/MW/yr; "the majority of their cost in O&M goes into module cleaning, manpower, and security"; semi-automatic robotic cleaning ~₹1.50 lakh/MW-DC/yr and automatic robotic ~₹1.20 lakh/MW-DC/yr — [Saur Energy](https://www.saurenergy.com/solar-energy-articles/indias-solar-om-market-a-tale-of-increased-domination-of-new-evolving-technologies)
- Heaven Green Energy glossary: comprehensive utility-scale O&M ₹4–8 lakh/MW/yr; "cleaning labour and water" ₹1,00,000–2,00,000/MW/yr; manual cleaning ₹8–12 lakh/yr for a 10 MW plant (≈₹0.8–1.2 lakh/MW/yr) — [O&M glossary](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/glossary/o-and-m); [cleaning blog](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/how-to-clean-solar-panels)
- GSE Renewables: ~₹5–8 lakh/MW/yr O&M, falling to ₹3–5 lakh/MW/yr at 5 MW+ — [GSE Renewables](https://gserenewables.com/blog/solar-operations-maintainance-services/)
- Taypro OPEX pricing for robotic cleaning ~₹800–1,500 per MW per cleaning cycle (vendor contract figure) — [Taypro June 2026 insights](https://taypro.in/insights/solar-cleaning-research-june-2026)
- Taypro's automatic cleaning robot is listed at ₹3,00,000 per unit on B2B marketplaces — [TradeIndia](https://www.tradeindia.com/products/25-kg-taypro-aluminium-solar-panel-automatic-cleaning-robot-7791456.html); [IndiaMART](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/aluminium-solar-panel-cleaning-automatic-robot-21619959930.html)
- NTPC Green's 2025 O&M tender for the 250 MW Shimbhu ki Bhurj-I project (Rajasthan) scopes module and inverter cleaning plus vegetation cutting, with no robotic/waterless method specified — [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/ntpc-green-floats-om-tender-for-250-mw-solar-project-in-rajasthan)

**Water consumption**
- Bridge to India found water use of slightly above 1 litre to as high as 4 litres per module per cleaning cycle — [Renewable Watch citing Bridge to India](https://renewablewatch.in/2020/12/03/waterless-way/)
- A Polywater/SPW calculation assumes 330 Wp modules cleaned twice monthly, giving ~218,160 litres/MW/yr (218 m³/MW/yr); manual wet cleaning 2–5 litres/module — [Polywater PDF](https://www.polywater.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/SPW-Intl-blog-Water-Use-IndiaCA.pdf)
- A CEEW-cited figure: up to 24,000 litres per wash for a 1 MW park of ~3,000 panels — [Inspire Clean Energy](https://inspire-ce.com/solar-power-in-developing-nations-brightening-lives/)
- CENFA Bhadla field study: 7–8 litres per panel wash — [CENFA PDF](https://www.cenfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Report_Solar-Power-in-India-A-Case-Study-of-the-Bhadla-Solar-Power-Park.pdf)
- CSTEP's NITI Aayog study notes solar PV needs "some water for cleaning" but does not quantify it — [NITI Aayog/CSTEP PDF](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2019-06/Final%20Report%20of%20the%20Research%20Study%20on%20Impact%20of%20Power%20Sector%20Growth%20on%20Water%20Resources%20conducted%20by%20C-STEP_Bangalore.pdf)
- Study flagging high water-use risk in the solar sector (2018) — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2018/10/03/study-high-risk-through-water-use-in-solar-sector/)
- Mercom: robotic cleaning "gaining traction in India amid water scarcity" — [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/robotic-cleaning-solar-modules-gaining-traction-india)

### Inferences
- Taking ~0.3%/day as a representative dry-season Rajasthan rate, a 14-day manual cycle implies ~2% average soiling loss; weekly cycles ~1%. At ~2,000 kWh/kWp/yr and PPA tariffs near ₹2.5/kWh, each 1% of soiling costs roughly ₹50,000/MW/yr of revenue; the ₹1–2 lakh/MW/yr cleaning spend plus ~₹1 lakh/MW/yr of residual loss sets the ceiling an alternative (coating/self-cleaning) must undercut. (Inference; tariff and yield assumptions are the researcher's.)
- Robotic cleaning at ₹1.2–1.5 lakh/MW/yr is already price-competitive with manual cleaning at ₹1–2 lakh/MW/yr in India, which is why the market has moved to robots so fast; a self-cleaning coating or passive product must therefore be benchmarked against Indian robot pricing (₹3 lakh/robot; ₹800–1,500/MW/cycle OPEX), not against manual cleaning in Western markets.
- Water quality (hard groundwater requiring RO/DI) and water-access politics (CENFA field evidence) make "waterless" a social-licence argument with large IPPs, not just a cost argument.

### Gaps
- No independent 2025-26 benchmark for manual cleaning labour cost per MW or daily wages of module cleaners was found; all per-MW cost figures are vendor or trade-press estimates and conflict with each other.
- No NISE, IIT Bombay or IIT Madras soiling study was surfaced; CSTEP's report does not quantify cleaning water. CEEW and NREL India soiling studies were referenced by vendor pages but could not be verified.
- No CGWA (Central Ground Water Authority) or Rajasthan state order restricting groundwater use for panel cleaning was found; whether such a regulatory restriction exists remains unverified.
- Typical cleaning frequency at Indian utility plants (e.g., weekly vs fortnightly) is not documented in a primary source here; only vendor statements.

## Key Question 3: Which robotic/waterless vendors have won Indian deployments, where, on what terms, and how

### Takeaway
India's robotic-cleaning market has flipped from Israeli pioneers (Ecoppia, Airtouch) to Chinese and Indian suppliers: Mercom's 2025 leaderboard has Sol-Bright (~35%), Solabot, LEAPTING, Aegeus and Vayu Solar holding ~82% of shipments, while LEAPTING has GW-scale orders from ReNew and Waaree; winning formulas were master supply agreements with one anchor IPP, repeat orders, specifying robots at the EPC/tracker design stage, and local manufacturing (Airtouch Noida, Ecoppia Punjab, LEAPTING planned).

### Cited Findings

- 2025 (Mercom India Solar Market Leaderboard 2026): Langfang Sol-Bright New Energy Technology ~35% of robotic cleaning equipment shipments; Solabot Technologies ~12%; LEAPTING ~12%; Aegeus Technologies >11%; Vayu Solar >11%; top five shipped ~82% — [Mercom India, 2025 leaderboard](https://www.mercomindia.com/top-robotic-solar-module-cleaning-equipment-suppliers-in-2025); [Now.solar summary, 15 Apr 2026](https://now.solar/2026/04/15/top-robotic-solar-module-cleaning-equipment-suppliers-in-2025-mercomindia-com/); [Mercom daily wrap](https://www.mercomindia.com/daily-news-wrap-up-leading-robotic-solar-module-cleaners-in-2025)
- 2024: Taypro led with 27.5%, followed by Sol-Bright 18.8% and Airtouch Solar 13%; LEAPTING 9.8%; Taypro supplied a 360 MW project in Rajasthan — [Mercom India, 2024 leaderboard](https://www.mercomindia.com/top-robotic-solar-module-cleaning-equipment-suppliers-in-2024)

## Key Question 5: Tender, registration and compliance steps for selling cleaning equipment/services

### Takeaway
Cleaning robots are not covered by ALMM (which governs modules and, from 1 Jun 2026, cells), but a foreign vendor faces: ~7.5% BCD + 10% surcharge + 18% IGST on robots classified under HS 8479.50 (~27–28.5% landed), a new BIS Quality Control Order effective 1 Oct 2026 that covers battery/electrical cleaning appliances, a GeM portal that requires an Indian entity (subsidiary or deemed-OEM distributor) to sell to PSUs, and PSU tenders (e.g., NTPC NETRA) restricted to "Class-I local suppliers" — all of which push toward an Indian legal presence and local assembly.

### Cited Findings
**Customs duty and GST**
- A pv magazine India commentary (28 Jul 2026) argues correctly classified cleaning robots fall under India's industrial-robot tariff heading, which carries a lighter duty than modules and cells, and notes a BIS quality-control order effective 1 Oct 2026 covering ~90 appliance categories including battery-operated cleaning equipment, with the certificate furnishable by the shipper or a licensed Indian representative (commentary, not an official ruling) — [pv magazine India, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.pv-magazine-india.com/2026/07/28/why-indias-renewable-energy-equipment-imports-are-getting-harder-not-easier/)
- HS 84795000 (industrial robots, n.e.s.): basic customs duty 7.5%, social welfare surcharge 10% of BCD, IGST 18%; effective total ~27.1–28.5% of assessable value (tariff aggregators; one site conflicts with 5%) — [Seair](https://www.seair.co.in/custom-duty/hs-code-84795000.aspx); [Eximpe](https://eximpe.com/hsncode-finder/84795000); [Treayo](https://treayo.com/en/hs/india/84795000); [Taxclue HSN 8479 GST](https://taxclue.in/hsn-code/8479)
- Module BCD figures conflict across sources: 40% modules / 25% cells vs. 20% for both (cited as effective Feb 2025) — [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/how-to-import-solar-panels-in-india); [Soleos Energy](https://www.soleosenergy.com/8-effects-of-import-duty-on-solar-panel-guide/); [Change Sourcing](https://change-sourcing.com/how-to-import-solar-panels-from-china-to-india/); [Energy Solutions policy page](https://energy-solutions.co/policy-intelligence/trade-and-tariffs/india-basic-customs-duty-bcd)

**BIS (QCO 2026)**
- The Safety of Household, Commercial and Similar Electrical Appliances (Quality Control) Order, 2026, notified 6 Apr 2026 by DPIIT (S.O. 1739(E)), requires >90 categories to obtain BIS certification and the ISI mark from 1 Oct 2026 (enterprises other than micro/small), 1 Jan 2027 (small), 1 Apr 2027 (micro); standard IS 302 (Part 1):2024 / IEC 60335-1:2020; the illustrative list names vacuum cleaners and water-suction cleaning appliances and spin extractors; the deadline was extended from March 2026 — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-electrical-appliances-qco-2026-bis-certification-guide-for-foreign-manufacturers-44541.html); [WorldTradeScanner on DPIIT Order S.O. 1739(E)](https://worldtradescanner.com/DPIIT%20Order%20S.O.%201739(E)%20dated%206%20April,%202026.htm); [ELP law note (PDF)](https://elplaw.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/BIS-Update-Extension-to-Compulsory-use-of-Standard-Mark-for-Household-Commercial-and-Similar-Electrical-Appliances.pdf); [The Week, 6 Apr 2026](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2026/04/06/govt-extends-implementation-timeline-for-quality-control-order-on-electrical-appliances-till-oct.html); [Jurishour](https://www.jurishour.in/notification/mandatory-bis-certification-household-commercial-electrical-appliances-october-1-2026/)
- BIS/ISI certification for vacuum cleaners and water-suction cleaning appliances under IS 302 (Part 1) — [Elitas RCS](https://mail.elitasrcs.com/products/bis-certification-for-vacuum-cleaners-and-water-suction-cleaning-appliances-is-302-part-1-iec-60335-1); [PSR Compliance](https://www.psrcompliance.com/blog/bis-isi-certification-vacuum-cleaners-india)

**ALMM**
- ALMM covers solar modules (List-I) and cells (List-II) for government-backed projects; List-II for cells took effect 1 Jun 2026, reiterated in an MNRE notice of 25 May 2026; an exemption window for net-metering/open-access projects runs to 31 Dec 2026 per an 18 Jul 2026 MNRE order (one source instead says List-II applies to those from 1 Jan 2027); government/PSU tenders assume full compliance — [TaiyangNews](https://taiyangnews.info/markets/india-enforces-almm-list-ii-for-solar-cells); [Heaven Green ALMM exemption note](https://www.heavengreenenergy.com/blog/almm-list-2-exemption-window-2026); [Websol Energy](https://www.websolenergy.com/almm-list-ii-for-solar-pv-cells-in-india-what-epcs-installers-module-manufacturers-need-to-know-in-2026/); [IEEFA on ALMM/import restrictions](https://ieefa.org/resources/how-import-restrictions-modules-will-push-solar-energy-tariffs-india)
- No source lists cleaning equipment as being within ALMM scope.

**GeM portal**
- Foreign companies cannot register on GeM using their overseas entity; they need a registered Indian presence (wholly owned subsidiary, JV or authorised Indian distributor), with the subsidiary described as the most common/recommended route; resellers need an OEM Authorisation Letter; products made outside India and sold by an Indian entity need "Deemed OEM" status; documents include PAN, GST, incorporation certificate and trademark certificate (or a notarised undertaking for unregistered brands) — [Bizfoc](https://bizfoc.com/oem-panel-foreign-companies-gem); [Corpseed deemed-OEM](https://admin.corpseed.com/service/deemed-oem-registration-on-gem-portal); [TenderShark OEM authorisation](https://www.tendershark.com/blogs/how-to-get-oem-authorization-certificate-for-gem-a-step-by-step-guide-2025); [Tata AIG GeM OEM/brand](https://www.tataaig.com/knowledge-center/sme-insurance/gem-portal-oem-and-brand-registration); official GeM General Terms and Conditions — [GeM GTC (PDF)](https://assets-bg.gem.gov.in/resources/pdf/GTC_on_GeM_v1.10_31Jan19.pdf); GeM 2024 vendor validation policy — [GeM policy (PDF)](https://assets-bg.gem.gov.in/resources/upload/shared_doc/gem-2024-vendor-validation-policy-v-3_1704955566.pdf); vendor assessment via RITES — [Professional Utilities](https://professionalutilities.com/gem-vendor-assessment)

**Local content in PSU tenders**
- NTPC's Feb 2026 tender for O&M of a 4 MW solar microgrid at its NETRA complex (Greater Noida) limits participation to Class-I local suppliers — [Mercom India](https://www.mercomindia.com/ntpc-floats-tender-for-om-of-4-mw-solar-projects-in-noida); [SolarQuarter, 5 Feb 2026](https://solarquarter.com/2026/02/05/ntpc-invites-bids-for-om-of-4-mw-solar-microgrid-project-in-greater-noida/)

**Payment norms (legal)**
- The MSMED Act caps payment to MSMEs at 45 days even if contracts say 60/90 days; MSEFC references must be decided within 90 days — [The Jackrabbit, 2026](https://thejackrabbit.in/msme-delayed-payment-india-2026/); the MSME Development (Amendment) Act, 2026 promises tighter timelines but its core dispute-resolution provisions have not been brought into force; a Parliamentary committee put delayed payments at ~₹8.1 lakh crore — [India Legal](https://indialegallive.com/column-news/a-promise-still-waiting-to-be-kept/); government delayed-payment portal — [MSME Samadhaan](https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/)

**Startup India recognition**
- Startup India/DPIIT guidance: holding and subsidiary companies "including foreign subsidiary" and entities incorporated outside India are not permitted for DPIIT recognition, and a recognised startup that becomes a subsidiary is derecognised — [Taxmann FAQs](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/faqs-dpiit-recognition-for-start-ups/); some consultancies claim foreign-owned Indian-incorporated companies can qualify (conflicting, less authoritative) — [Ebizfiling](https://ebizfiling.com/blog/register-foreign-company-under-startup-india/); [BSamrish](https://bsamrishindia.com/startup-india-registration-can-foreign-companies-and-foreign-shareholders-participate/)

### Inferences
- A self-cleaning *coating* (chemical) would not be an "electrical appliance" under the 2026 QCO, but any powered robot, brush or sprayer almost certainly is; the coating route avoids BIS but may raise separate chemical/BIS-standard questions not researched here.
- Private IPP purchases (the bulk of the market, per Q3) do not require GeM; GeM/Class-I rules matter for NTPC, NHPC, SJVN, SECI-owned and state-nodal-agency (GUVNL, RUVNL, KREDL, GEDA, MSEDCL) plants, which is a secondary but growing segment (NTPC Green at Khavda, SJVN, NHPC).
- The combined effect of ~27% landed duty on robots, BIS marking, GeM's Indian-entity rule and Class-I local-supplier clauses explains why every top-five robot supplier either manufactures in India or plans to.

### Inferences (practitioner advice — not verified by a source in this session)
- Introductions: warm introductions (via an EPC, an existing O&M contractor, a consultant such as Mercom/JMK/Bridge to India, or a trade body) outperform cold outreach; the first meeting is for rapport and credibility, not for a quote.
- Calendar: avoid launching outreach or expecting decisions in the Diwali fortnight (late Oct/Nov, date varies) and the last two weeks of March (fiscal year-end on 31 March, when finance teams close books); conversely, Jan–Feb is when next-year O&M budgets are being set, and the REI (Oct) and Intersolar India (Feb/Mar) windows bracket that cycle. Indian national holidays (Republic Day 26 Jan, Independence Day 15 Aug, Holi in March) also break weeks.
- Demo expectations: expect to be asked for a free or heavily discounted site pilot (one block, one dry season, PR measured against a control block by the IPP's own ENOC/analytics team), followed by a reference call with another Indian IPP before an MSA.
- Price: expect anchoring to Chinese robot quotes and requests for INR pricing, deferred payment, retention (typically 5–10% held until performance guarantee), and local AMC/spares commitments; expect negotiation to continue after "agreement."
- Channels: email for formal documents; WhatsApp for day-to-day follow-up once a relationship exists (ask permission first, keep messages short, and do not send decks over WhatsApp before the first meeting).
- These points are consistent with the hierarchy/relationship findings above but are the researcher's synthesis; the report should label them as practitioner guidance.

**Entry structures (legal/advisory sources)**
- An Indian subsidiary is the usual route for sustained commercial operations and can perform all commercial activity; a liaison office is limited to representation/market research/coordination, cannot earn income, must file Annual Activity Certificates with the RBI and must strictly adhere to its permitted activities; a branch office suits testing the market or time-limited project engagements; with a distributor, the risk is the Indian partner becoming a "dependent agent or de facto India office" (permanent-establishment risk), so contract boundaries must be documented — [India Briefing](https://india-briefing.com/doing-business-guide/india/company-establishment/representative-office-branch-office-project-office-vs-llp-s-and-subsidiary-in-india); [Treelife](https://treelife.in/compliance/how-can-a-foreign-company-enter-india); [SetMyCompany checklist](https://setmycompany.com/foreign-company-india-entry-subsidiary-branch-liaison-office-checklist/); [IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/subsidiary-vs-branch-vs-liaison-office-india); [Maheshwari & Co](https://www.maheshwariandco.com/faq/business-structures-for-foreign-companies-in-india/); Dezan Shira market-entry masterclass — [Dezan Shira](https://www.dezshira.com/events/details/india-market-entry-masterclass-choosing-the-right-business-structure-for-expansion-into-india-10382.html)

### Inferences
- For a *coating/passive* self-cleaning product, the lowest-friction route is a licensing or co-marketing deal with one of the top-five O&M providers (Mitarsh, Inox Green, Jakson Green, Inspire, S&W), who own the cleaning crews and PR guarantees and can bundle the product into existing contracts without the customer running a separate procurement.
- For a *robot/mechanical* product, the precedent set by Airtouch (subsidiary + local assembly) and the regulatory stack (BIS, GeM Indian-entity, ~27% landed duty) make an Indian subsidiary or a licensing/contract-manufacturing deal with Taypro/Aegeus/Solabot-type firms the realistic options; a pure distributor model exposes the vendor to PE risk and leaves it unable to bid PSU tenders.
- Government startup programmes are effectively closed to foreign-owned entities, so "access programmes" for a foreign vendor are really: (a) corporate pilots (Tata Power, Adani ENOC, ReNew), (b) trade-fair matchmaking (Intersolar India/REI), (c) bilateral channels (India–EFTA summit was the venue for the Tata–Ocean Sun deal; Israel–India links underpin Airtouch/Ecoppia).
