## KQ1. Who are the decision-makers and gatekeepers for O&M/cleaning technology at IPPs, utilities, EPCs and O&M contractors, and what are their KPIs and fears?

### Takeaway
Documented sources place the **asset manager** (commercial/financial optimisation, supplier selection, insurance compliance) and the **O&M contractor / plant O&M manager** (technical, energy-output optimisation, contract and performance-guarantee enforcement) at the centre of O&M-technology decisions, with procurement (vendor viability), insurers (policy compliance), module OEMs (warranty) and independent engineers (lender sign-off) as gatekeepers; generic B2B research says a complex purchase now involves 6–10 (Gartner) to 13 (Forrester) stakeholders. The dominant, documented fear specific to cleaning technology is **module surface damage (anti-reflective coating wear, micro-cracks) and the resulting warranty exposure** — PI Berlin has shown robots *can* cause long-term module damage, and tenders now ask for independent cleaning-resistance evidence.

### Inferences
- For a cleaning/self-cleaning vendor the practical DMU at an IPP is: **champion** = Head/VP of O&M or Asset Management (owns PR/availability and the O&M budget line); **technical gate** = plant/O&M manager and the incumbent O&M contractor (who may see a robot or coating as a threat to its cleaning scope or as a way to de-risk its performance-ratio guarantee); **commercial gate** = procurement (vendor viability, insurance certificates); **external gates** = module OEM (warranty letter), insurer (maintenance-log evidence), and the lender's IE (if the asset is financed or being refinanced). This mapping is inferred from the role descriptions above, not from a single source.
- Because the asset manager is explicitly responsible for insurance-policy compliance of site equipment, a vendor that cannot show OEM warranty compatibility and insurer-acceptable documentation is likely to be blocked at the asset-manager level before procurement is even engaged.
- The Mordor claim that performance-ratio O&M contracts push soiling risk onto O&M providers implies the **O&M contractor** can be a buyer in its own right (to protect its PR guarantee), not just a blocker.

### Gaps
- No source was found that explicitly maps sign-off authority (who approves spend above what threshold) for O&M technology at IPPs/EPCs/O&M firms; the Solar Best Practices guidelines and the NREL/OSTI "Budgeting for Solar PV Plant O&M: Practices and Pricing" report ([OSTI 1234935](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1234935)) are the best candidates but could not be opened.
- No documented data on CTO/Chief Innovation Officer vs CFO involvement in O&M purchases specifically; the utility guides discuss "innovation departments" only generically.
- No primary text of the PV Tech/PI Berlin damage study (damage mechanism, which brush types) was retrievable; the Solaris and Ecoppia warranty/certification claims are vendor statements and were not independently verified.
- Module OEM cleaning manuals (e.g., Jinko, LONGi, Trina) specifying permitted brush types/pressure for warranty validity were not retrieved (search budget exhausted before this query).

## KQ2. Documented buying triggers, procurement cycles and budget calendars (fiscal year, capex vs opex, O&M contract terms), with regional notes

### Takeaway
Documented triggers are (i) O&M contract renewal/retender windows (initial terms are typically 5 years for C&I, 5–15 years utility-scale, with owners advised to benchmark or retender every 3–5 years), (ii) measured underperformance against P50 (8.6% US average in 2025), (iii) water scarcity and water-use restrictions (Middle East, North Africa, India, US Southwest; SolarPower Europe guidelines call for minimising cleaning water), and (iv) performance-ratio-based O&M contracts that shift soiling risk to the O&M provider. O&M budgets are commonly planned at ~1–2% of capex per year, O&M is ~77% of utility-scale OPEX, and robotic cleaning is generally treated as capex (owned fleet) or opex (per-clean / per-MWh / lease service), with service models preferred in C&I.

### Inferences
- The highest-probability timing windows for a cleaning-tech vendor are: (a) 12–18 months before a 5-year O&M term expiry (owner benchmarking/retender), (b) the first annual performance review after COD when actual-vs-P50 gaps are first quantified, and (c) annual O&M budget setting (the 1–2%-of-capex planning cycle) — inferred from the contract-term and budgeting sources above.
- Classification matters for the pitch: an owned-fleet (capex) sale competes with scarce capex and may need IE/lender review at refinancing, whereas a per-clean/per-MWh service fits an existing opex line (the "existing budget category" that utility guides say avoids stalls). Vendors are already converging on service/lease models in C&I for this reason.
- In India and the Gulf, procurement is formally gated by local-content and local-supplier rules (Class-I local supplier for NTPC; IKTVA/ICV in KSA/UAE), so a foreign small vendor realistically needs a local manufacturing/assembly or JV partner before it can even bid on PSU/national-champion tenders.

### Gaps
- No source on how solar owners' **fiscal-year calendars** (e.g., Indian FY April–March, calendar-year US IPPs) interact with O&M budget approval; no source on capitalisation thresholds or lease accounting (IFRS 16 / ASC 842) treatment of cleaning robots.
- No documented examples of **refinancing, ESG/water-use reporting, labour-cost spikes or module-warranty events** acting as explicit purchase triggers for cleaning tech (searches on these were either not run — budget exhausted — or returned only generic marketing).
- The named Middle-East "water-use regulations" favouring waterless cleaning were not identified; the Indian groundwater-restriction search (Rajasthan/CGWA) was not executed.
- Quantified labour-cost data for manual cleaning (per MW, by region) was not found; sources only assert robots "lower manual labor expenses".

## KQ3. How pilot programmes are structured (paid vs free, MW size, duration, success metrics, data-sharing, conversion terms) and which structures convert

### Takeaway
Documented solar-specific pilots are small (≈1 MW section at Enel's Totana plant; vendor guidance of a 5 MW block) and run on the owner's site at the owner's cost in corporate-challenge formats (Iberdrola PERSEO bore pilot costs and offered "official global supplier" status on success). Cross-industry practitioner literature converges on the same conversion rules: a named economic owner, written success criteria and a fixed window agreed before launch, a pre-agreed price, and a conversion clause committing to a commercial contract if KPIs are met. The best documented conversion data is EDP's Free Electrons: >60 pilots (≈€2M total) → >10 startups with commercial contracts worth >€35M.

## KQ4. Role of independent engineers, insurers and lenders in blocking or enabling new O&M tech, and how a vendor becomes "bankable"

### Takeaway
The lender's independent engineer (selected by the lender, paid by the developer) reviews technology, O&M plans/budgets and annual O&M performance, and a SurgePV guide explicitly advises engaging an IE "before using unfamiliar technology"; IEs have historically mis-estimated soiling versus on-site data (kWh Analytics 2021), which is both a risk and an opening. For cleaning equipment specifically, the emerging bankability proxy is **independent lab certification of module-surface compatibility (PI Berlin's accelerated 3,100-cycle sand test; Ecoppia's nine-brand certification)** plus written module-OEM warranty acceptance; insurers add a documentation gate (maintenance logs, policy-compliant equipment, carrier-rated certificates of insurance).

### Cited Findings
**Independent engineer role and scope**
- [Fact] The IE is the lender's technical representative, "typically selected by the lender but paid by the developer/owner" to maintain independence; typical scope includes "fatal flaw and/or technology reviews" and O&M review items, including annual O&M reviews — [Wikipedia: Independent engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_engineer)
- [Practitioner opinion] Hire an IE before financial close on C&I/utility projects "and also before using unfamiliar technology"; lenders generally require an IE report at financial close — [SurgePV, Solar lender engineer 2026](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-lender-engineer); [SurgePV, Solar independent engineer 2026](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-independent-engineer)
- [Fact, consultancy scope] Lender's technical advisory "reviews yield, grid, design, permits, contracts, technology, construction programme, O&M assumptions and risk allocation" — [SgurrEnergy LTA](https://sgurrenergy.com/services/technical-advisory/lenders-technical-advisory)
- [Practitioner opinion] IFC's IE framework covers energy assessment, technology assessment, EPC contractor assessment and O&M plan review; IREDA's IE scope includes an O&M budget assessment — [Heaven Designs, DFI-bankable solar engineering](https://heavendesigns.in/blog/dfi-bankable-solar-engineering-afdb-ifc-usaid/) (secondary; verify against IFC/IREDA documents)
- [Practitioner opinion] Lenders may ask for reproducible model files, traceable weather data, uncertainty/probability analysis, sensitivities, independent review and "defined reliance" for yield models — [SurgePV, Bankable PVsyst reports](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/pvsyst-report-bankability-guide)
- [Practitioner opinion] Owner's engineer vs independent engineer distinctions — [Exactus Energy](https://www.exactusenergy.com/blog/independent-engineering-versus-owners-engineering); [RCT Solutions](https://www.rct-solutions.com/projects/owners-engineering-vs-lenders-engineering/)
- [Fact] kWh Analytics (2021): IE soiling estimates diverged from on-site measurements — [SRA 2021](https://kwhanalytics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kWhAnalytics_SolarRiskAssessment21_06-08-21.pdf)

**Independent testing / certification as the bankability proxy for cleaning tech**
- [Fact] PI Berlin: tender bidders now present independent confirmation of surface cleaning resistance; PI Berlin tests both modules and robot producers — [pv magazine press release](https://www.pv-magazine.com/press-releases/photovoltaik-institut-berlin-verifies-impact-of-cleaning-robots_100022221/)
- [Fact] Standardized test: 3,100 accelerated cleaning cycles with sand on float glass (verify) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336718524_STANDARDIZED_TEST_PROCEDURE_FOR_PV_MODULE_CLEANING_EQUIPMENT)

- [Fact, academic] A 2023 arXiv study examined the deflection imposed on PV panels by a cleaning robot — [arXiv 2306.05340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05340)

**Insurers**
- [Vendor claim] Insurers "now demand granular documentation of maintenance logs" when shifting from manual to automated cleaning — [Taypro](https://taypro.in/blog/insurance-considerations-for-robotic-solar-cleaning-assets)
- [Fact] Asset managers must verify site equipment meets policy requirements or coverage may be ineffective — [Solar Best Practices AM guidelines](https://solarbestpractices.com/guidelines/detail/asset-management) (attribution per search summary)
- [Fact] kWh Analytics' Solar Risk Assessment is distributed to and summarised for insurance brokers — [Broker takeaways](https://kwhanalytics.com/brokers-key-takeaways-from-kwh-analytics-2025-solar-risk-assessment/); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-management/solar-risks-in-2025-report-flags-bess-and-pv-operational-gaps-538557.aspx)
- [Fact] Example contractual insurance gates: a NY State solar solicitation requires the prime contractor to make each subcontractor procure required policies "prior to the commencement of any work" and sets a carrier-rating floor — [NY OGS solar attachment (2026 PDF)](https://ogs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/04/0530223378attachment04_insurance.pdf); a school-district O&M agreement sets $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate GL and minimum A:VII insurer rating — [Redwood City O&M Agreement](https://go.boarddocs.com/ca/redwood/Board.nsf/files/DAHTCU76C4A6/$file/Solar%20System%20Operations%20and%20Maintenance%20Agreement.pdf); a financier's partner-onboarding checklist requires $1M umbrella/excess, E&O, background check and safety manual — [Palmetto partner onboarding](https://help.palmetto.finance/en/articles/13974536-compliance-solar-energy-plan-partner-onboarding-requirements)

**Cybersecurity (if the cleaning system is connected)**
- [Fact] Travelers: a compromised third-party monitoring system could disrupt an entire portfolio; use clear contract language on responsibilities — [Travelers](https://www.travelers.com/resources/business-topics/cyber-security/cyber-risks-for-solar-and-wind-installations)
- [Practitioner opinion] Contractor cyber guidance: subcontractor devices on site networks should meet minimum standards (current AV, supported OS, encrypted storage); separate credentials per vendor; revoke access at project end; cyber insurers look for MFA, EDR, phishing training, tested backups, IR plan — [Attain Technology (Sep 2026)](https://attaintechnology.com/2026/09/30/the-complete-guide-to-managed-cybersecurity-for-ri-contractors/)
- [Practitioner opinion] Have security documentation ready before utility procurement begins — [Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/utility-sales/) (attribution per search summary)

### Inferences
- For a self-cleaning coating or robot, "bankability" in practice decomposes into four artefacts a vendor must assemble: (1) an independent lab report on module-surface/AR-coating compatibility (PI Berlin or equivalent; TÜV/UL-type bodies were not found doing this for cleaning equipment in retrievable sources), (2) written module-OEM warranty non-voiding letters for the specific module models on the target site, (3) a maintenance-log/data trail acceptable to the insurer, and (4) an IE-reviewable soiling-loss and energy-gain model with a measured control. This is inferred from the gate descriptions above; no single source lists all four.
- Because the IE reviews O&M *budgets* and *annual* performance, the IE is most influential at financial close and refinancing; for an operating, already-financed asset, the asset manager/insurer gates are likely the binding ones day-to-day.

### Gaps
- No lender policy, IFC guide text, or IE firm statement (DNV, Black & Veatch, UL Solutions, TÜV, Enertis, Leidos) on how unproven O&M technology is treated (e.g., required MW-years of track record, reserve adjustments) was retrievable; a search on this was not executed because the budget ran out.
- No evidence found of insurers formally endorsing or rejecting robotic/self-cleaning methods; the only "insurer demands" claim is from a vendor.
- No examples of IEs including or excluding automated-cleaning energy gains in P50 models.

## KQ6. What B2B cleantech/industrial sales research says gets a small vendor a first meeting and a signed pilot (champions, ROI calculators, references, risk-reversal, performance-based pricing); use of Challenger/MEDDIC/"chasm" frameworks

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- The combination of "reliability first" + "cleaning cadence decides awards" + the documented warranty/damage fear suggests the first-meeting hook for a self-cleaning vendor is **risk removal evidence** (lab certification, OEM letters, control-block data from a reference site), with ROI calculators as secondary; the sources on pilot conversion suggest the pilot offer itself should carry the pre-agreed price and conversion clause to avoid "pilot purgatory".

## KQ7. Common reasons new O&M-tech vendors fail to convert pilots into contracts

### Takeaway
Documented failure modes are: utility pilots that "never land" while the startup burns cash waiting (Latitude Media), piloting with the wrong customer, no named owner/economic buyer, no written success criteria or pre-agreed price, no conversion clause, treating the innovation department as the buyer, no commercial team separate from the validation team, running many pilots that none convert, misalignment with an existing budget category, and procurement/legal delays. Cleaning-specific risk factors documented separately are module-damage evidence (PI Berlin) and contract/warranty language that does not explicitly permit robotic/waterless methods.

### Cited Findings
- [Practitioner / trade press] Utility pilots can stall indefinitely, leaving startups running out of money while they wait — [Latitude Media, "Welcome to utility pilot purgatory"](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/pilot-purgatory-the-quintessential-definition-of-insanity/)
- [Practitioner opinion] A startup with "dozens of pilot programs running but none of them converting" should be willing to walk away — [IamBenSchmidt glossary](https://iambenschmidt.com/glossary/what-is-a-pilot-program/)
- [Practitioner opinion] Wrong-customer pilots are harder to convert and often indicate poor market fit — [TechCrunch (2022)](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/07/3-ways-deep-tech-founders-can-climb-out-of-pilot-purgatory/)
- [Practitioner opinion] Missing owner / missing written success measures — [Caymont Ventures](https://insights.caymont.com/p/the-pilot-paradox)
- [Practitioner opinion] No separate commercial team for pricing, procurement, contract structure — [Rebel Strategy Lab](https://rebelstrategylab.com/diagnosis/pilot-purgatory/)
- [Practitioner opinion] Innovation department ≠ closing; budget-category misalignment; single-threaded contact; procurement/legal delays — [The Impact](https://readtheimpact.com/why-clean-energy-startups-underestimate-selling-to-utilities/); [Enduring Planet](https://enduringplanet.com/insights/selling-to-utilities-as-a-climate-startup); [Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/utility-sales/) (attribution per search summary)
- [Fact] PI Berlin: robots can cause long-term module damage; tenders require independent cleaning-resistance evidence — [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/cleaning-robots-can-cause-long-term-module-damage-finds-pi-berlin/); [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/press-releases/photovoltaik-institut-berlin-verifies-impact-of-cleaning-robots_100022221/)
- [Vendor claim] O&M contracts and OEM warranty terms may not explicitly permit robotic/waterless cleaning; insurers want granular logs — [Taypro](https://taypro.in/blog/insurance-considerations-for-robotic-solar-cleaning-assets)
- [Fact] Corporate challenges show long or unreported conversion: Iberdrola's 2019 cleaning pilots have no retrievable outcome report; EDP's Free Electrons converts roughly 1 in 6 pilots to contracts (>60 pilots → >10 contracts) — [Iberdrola](https://www.iberdrola.com/en/inovacao/entrevista-vencedores-limpeza-paineis-fotovoltaicos); [ESB](https://esb.ie/news---insights/blog/article/esb/2025/01/14/free-electrons--unlocking-the-power-of-global-energy-innovation)
- [Fact] US federal policy changes have reduced traditional energy-innovation funding (Oct 2025), a macro headwind for US utility pilots — [GridCatalyst](https://gridcatalyst.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/accelerating-utility-innovation-and-startup-collaboration.pdf)

### Inferences
- Mapping the generic failure modes to a self-cleaning vendor: the most likely conversion killers are (1) the incumbent O&M contractor (whose cleaning scope/revenue is displaced) not being made a beneficiary, (2) the pilot measuring "it works" rather than incremental kWh vs a control block at an agreed price, and (3) the vendor never clearing the warranty/insurance gates so the asset manager cannot approve scale-up even if the champion wants to. These are inferences from the role and gate evidence in KQ1 and KQ4.

### Gaps
- No quantitative survey of pilot-to-contract conversion rates in solar O&M technology; Latitude Media and GridCatalyst (likely to contain such data) were not readable.
- No documented post-mortems from cleaning-robot or coating vendors on lost deals.
